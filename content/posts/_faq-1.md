## how-hydraulic-cylinders-work
### Why does my cylinder extend slower than it retracts?
Because the effective area is different on each side of the piston. When extending, oil fills the full bore area; when retracting, it fills only the annulus — the bore area minus the rod area. The same pump flow therefore fills the smaller annulus faster, so the rod retracts more quickly. This is normal behaviour. If the difference seems larger than expected, or extension has slowed over time, check for pump wear, internal leakage or flow control settings rather than assuming the cylinder is at fault.
### Can I increase a cylinder's force by raising the pressure?
In theory, yes — force rises in proportion to pressure. In practice you must stay within the rated pressure of every component in the circuit: the cylinder, hoses, valves and pump. Raising the relief setting beyond these ratings risks seal extrusion, burst hoses, cracked welds and serious injury. If you need more force, the safer route is usually a larger bore cylinder or a design review by an engineer who can confirm the structure and mountings can also carry the higher load.
### What is the difference between bore, rod and stroke?
The bore is the inside diameter of the barrel, which determines the piston area and therefore the pushing force. The rod is the diameter of the piston rod, which affects pulling force, buckling strength and retract speed. The stroke is the distance the rod travels between fully retracted and fully extended. These three figures, together with the mountings and working pressure, are the core of any cylinder specification and are the first things a manufacturer will ask for.
### How often should hydraulic cylinders be inspected?
Operators should check for leaks, rod damage and unusual movement as part of daily or pre-use checks. A more detailed inspection of rods, mountings, pins and hoses fits naturally into monthly or quarterly maintenance, depending on duty. Cylinders on critical or safety-related equipment, such as cranes, should also be covered by the equipment's periodic thorough examination. The right frequency depends on operating hours, environment and the consequences of failure, so adjust it based on what your inspections find.

## tie-rod-vs-welded-hydraulic-cylinders
### Can I replace a tie-rod cylinder with a welded cylinder?
Often, yes — provided the replacement matches the stroke, retracted length, mountings, port positions and pressure rating. A welded cylinder is usually more compact, so it can fit in the same space. Check that the mounting dimensions are identical or that adapters are acceptable, and confirm that the welded design is serviceable enough for your maintenance approach. If the original tie-rod cylinder followed a dimensional standard, discuss the change with your supplier so future replacements remain straightforward.
### Why do tie-rod cylinders sometimes leak at the end caps?
Under high pressure or shock loading, the tie rods stretch slightly. This can allow the end caps to separate microscopically from the barrel, letting the static seals extrude or leak. Correct tie-rod torque, proper static seals and keeping pressure peaks within the design rating all help. If leakage keeps returning, the application may simply be too severe for a tie-rod design, and a welded or mill-type cylinder could be the better long-term choice.
### Are welded cylinders harder to repair?
They require more specialist tooling but are very repairable. Glands may be threaded, retained by wire rings or bolted, and years of service can make them tight to remove. An experienced repair shop has the equipment to dismantle them without damage, inspect every component, re-seal, and pressure test. The main difference for maintenance teams is that welded cylinders are less suited to quick resealing on site, so holding a reconditioned spare is often the best strategy for critical machines.
### Which design is more cost-effective?
For standard industrial applications at moderate pressure, catalogue tie-rod cylinders are usually the most economical option, particularly in common sizes. For mobile, high-pressure or space-limited applications, welded cylinders give better value because they last longer in those conditions. Consider total cost: a cheaper cylinder that leaks or fails repeatedly costs more in downtime and repairs than a slightly more expensive design suited to the duty.

## telescopic-hydraulic-cylinders-explained
### Why do telescopic cylinders extend in stages?
The stage with the largest effective area needs the lowest pressure to move, so it extends first. Each smaller stage needs progressively higher pressure, so they follow in sequence. On retraction under gravity, the order is generally reversed. If stages extend out of sequence, the cause may be friction from wear or contamination, damaged stop rings, or internal leakage, and the cylinder should be inspected before it causes uneven loading or damage.
### Can a telescopic cylinder be repaired if one stage is bent?
A bent stage normally needs replacing rather than straightening, because straightening rarely restores the precise geometry and surface finish needed for sealing. The rest of the cylinder can usually be reconditioned with new seals, bearings and stop rings. It is equally important to find out why the stage bent — side loading from tipping on uneven ground or sticky loads is a common cause — otherwise the repaired cylinder may fail the same way.
### How do I bleed air from a telescopic cylinder?
Follow the equipment manufacturer's procedure. Typically, the cylinder is extended slowly through several full cycles at low load, and bleed screws, where fitted, are opened carefully to release air. Air causes jerky, spongy movement and can damage seals, so it is worth bleeding thoroughly after any repair or hose change. Always make sure the load body is safely supported and that nobody is in the danger zone during bleeding.
### What information do I need to order a replacement telescopic cylinder?
Record the number of stages, the retracted and fully extended lengths, the diameter of the largest stage, mounting types and dimensions at both ends, port size and position, working pressure, and the body or load it lifts. Photographs of the mountings and any nameplate are very helpful. If the original failed, explain how, so the manufacturer can consider improvements such as heavier stages or better bearings.

## hydraulic-cylinder-failure-causes
### What is the most common cause of hydraulic cylinder failure?
Contamination is the most common underlying cause across hydraulic systems in general. Hard particles score rods and barrels and wear seals, while water causes corrosion. Many failures that appear to be "seal problems" actually start with dirt entering past a worn wiper or during maintenance. Good filtration, sealed reservoirs, clean maintenance practices and healthy wipers prevent a large share of cylinder failures.
### How can I tell if side loading is damaging my cylinder?
Look for uneven wear: an oval-shaped wear pattern on the rod or guide bushing, wear concentrated on one side of the piston wear rings, or seals that start leaking soon after replacement. Visually, you may notice the cylinder flexing or mountings showing uneven wear. Check alignment between the mounting points through the full stroke, and consider spherical bearings where some misalignment is unavoidable.
### Should I replace all seals or just the leaking one?
Replace the complete seal set. Seals in a cylinder age together, and if one has failed the others are usually near the end of their life too. Dismantling a cylinder is the expensive part of the job, so fitting a full kit avoids a second repair a few weeks later. While the cylinder is apart, inspect the rod, barrel and wear rings, because damaged surfaces will quickly destroy new seals.
### When is a cylinder failure a safety issue?
Any cylinder that supports or controls a load — on cranes, lifting platforms, presses or tipping bodies — becomes a safety issue if it can drift, drop or fail suddenly. External leaks of high-pressure oil can also cause injection injuries and fire risks near hot surfaces. Take such equipment out of service until it is repaired, support loads mechanically before working on them, and follow the equipment's inspection requirements after repair.

## hydraulic-cylinder-seal-types
### What does a wiper seal do if it does not seal pressure?
The wiper protects the rod seal. As the rod retracts, the wiper scrapes off dust, mud, water and debris that would otherwise be dragged into the gland. A worn or damaged wiper is one of the most common reasons rod seals fail early, especially on mobile and outdoor equipment. Heavy-duty double-lip wipers or metal-cased scrapers are available for very dirty environments.
### Are polyurethane or PTFE seals better?
Neither is better in every case. Polyurethane offers excellent wear and extrusion resistance and is very common in mobile hydraulics. PTFE-based seals have very low friction and suit high speeds and applications where stick-slip must be avoided, but they need careful installation and usually an energiser. The right choice depends on pressure, speed, temperature, fluid and environment — which is why seal selection should be based on actual operating conditions.
### Why do new seals sometimes leak immediately after a rebuild?
Common causes include seals damaged during installation by sharp edges or threads, a scratched rod or barrel, incorrect groove dimensions, the wrong seal profile, or contamination left inside the cylinder. Occasionally a seal is installed the wrong way round. Proper tools, deburred components, careful cleaning and a pressure test after assembly catch most of these problems before the cylinder returns to service.
### How long should hydraulic seals last?
There is no single figure. Seal life depends on operating hours, pressure, speed, temperature, oil cleanliness, rod condition and alignment. In clean, well-maintained systems seals can last many years; in contaminated or overheated systems they may fail within months. Rather than relying on time, monitor leakage and drift and use those observations, plus findings from previous repairs, to plan resealing.

## how-to-measure-hydraulic-cylinder
### Can I measure bore size without dismantling the cylinder?
Not exactly. You can measure the outside diameter of the barrel and estimate the bore from typical wall thicknesses, but this is only an estimate. Many bore and rod sizes follow standard increments, which helps narrow it down. For an accurate replacement, tell the manufacturer that you measured the outside diameter, or allow them to dismantle the cylinder and measure the bore directly during inspection.
### What is the difference between stroke and closed length?
Stroke is how far the rod moves. Closed (retracted) length is the distance between the mounting centres when the cylinder is fully retracted. Both are needed: the stroke defines the motion, and the closed length defines whether the cylinder fits the machine geometry. The extended length should equal the closed length plus the stroke, which is a useful check on your measurements.
### How do I identify port thread types?
Use thread gauges and calipers to measure the outside diameter and thread pitch, and look for features such as an O-ring face or chamfer. Common types include BSP, metric, NPT and SAE O-ring boss, and some cylinders use flange ports instead. Photograph the ports and note their orientation relative to the mountings. If you are unsure, send photos and measurements to the manufacturer.
### Is it better to send the old cylinder to the manufacturer?
If downtime allows, yes. With the cylinder in hand, the manufacturer can measure every dimension accurately, inspect the failure, check the materials and recommend improvements. This removes the risk of measurement errors. If the machine cannot wait, send detailed measurements and photos for a replacement, then send the old cylinder afterwards so the drawing can be confirmed and kept on file.

## repair-or-replace-hydraulic-cylinder
### How long does a cylinder repair take compared with a new cylinder?
Repairs are often quicker, especially for custom or large cylinders, because the main structure already exists. Typical repair time depends on what is found: resealing is fast, while re-chroming a rod or honing a barrel adds processing time. New custom cylinders require materials, machining, welding and testing, which usually takes longer. Ask your supplier for both options and their realistic timelines when deciding.
### Is a repaired cylinder as reliable as a new one?
A professionally repaired cylinder — with components inspected and measured, worn parts restored or replaced, new seals fitted and a pressure test performed — can perform reliably for a long time. Reliability depends on the quality of the repair and on fixing the root cause of the failure. A quick reseal without inspection or testing is far less reliable.
### What should a repair findings report include?
It should describe the condition of the rod, barrel, piston, gland, seals and mountings, the measurements taken, what was replaced or repaired, the likely cause of failure, and the test results. Photographs of damage are valuable. This information helps you correct problems in the machine, such as misalignment or contamination, and provides a record for future maintenance.
### When does it make sense to upgrade during a repair?
Upgrade when the failure points to a design weakness or a change in duty: a bent rod suggests a larger rod diameter, recurring corrosion suggests a better rod coating, and overheating seals suggest a higher-temperature material. Because the cylinder is already apart, upgrades during repair are often inexpensive compared with the benefit of longer service life.

## hard-chrome-plating-piston-rods
### Can a scratched piston rod be polished instead of re-chromed?
Light surface marks that have not penetrated the chrome can sometimes be polished out. Deeper scratches, pits or areas where the chrome is chipped or worn through cannot be fixed by polishing, because the protective layer is already compromised. In those cases the rod should be re-chromed or replaced. Fitting new seals over a damaged rod will lead to repeat leaks.
### Why is induction hardening done before chrome plating?
Chrome is hard but thin. If the steel underneath is soft, an impact can dent the base material and crack the chrome. Induction hardening gives the rod a hard outer case that supports the chrome and resists dents. Together, the hardened case and chrome layer provide much better protection than chrome on a soft base.
### What causes rust spots on chrome rods?
Chrome plating contains microscopic cracks or porosity. In humid, salty or chemical environments, moisture can reach the steel through these paths and cause pitting, especially when rods stay extended while equipment is idle. Retracting rods during storage, cleaning off salt and chemicals, applying protective products and specifying upgraded coatings for harsh environments all reduce corrosion.
### Are there alternatives to hard chrome?
Yes. Options include nickel-chrome systems, stainless steel rods, thermal spray coatings and laser cladding. These are typically used in marine, offshore, chemical and other highly corrosive environments. They usually cost more than standard chrome, so they are best reserved for applications where corrosion is the main reason rods fail. Discuss compatibility with seals and surface finish requirements with your cylinder manufacturer.

## hydraulic-cylinder-drift-diagnosis
### Is some drift normal?
Some drift can be expected in circuits that rely only on a spool-type directional valve to hold a load, because spool valves have small internal clearances. Applications that must hold loads securely — cranes, lifting platforms and presses — use load-holding valves such as counterbalance or pilot-operated check valves to minimise drift. Any noticeable change in drift rate is a signal to investigate.
### Why does drift get worse when the oil is hot?
Hot oil is thinner, so it leaks more easily through worn seals and valve clearances. If drift is acceptable in the morning but worsens after hours of operation, internal leakage in cylinders or valves is likely, and you should also check why the oil is getting hot. Excessive temperatures accelerate seal wear and oil degradation.
### Can a contaminated valve cause drift?
Yes. A particle trapped on the seat of a check valve or counterbalance valve can prevent it from closing fully, allowing oil to escape and the load to drift. Sometimes cleaning the valve solves the problem, but the source of contamination should also be addressed with better filtration and clean maintenance practices.
### Is it safe to test a cylinder for internal leakage on the machine?
Only if done by trained personnel following the equipment manufacturer's procedures, with loads lowered or mechanically supported and pressures controlled. Disconnecting hoses from a cylinder that is holding a load is dangerous. Where there is any doubt, remove the cylinder and test it on a proper test bench, which also gives a more reliable result.

## what-is-a-hydraulic-power-pack
### What is the difference between a power pack and a hydraulic power unit?
The terms are used interchangeably. Both describe a self-contained unit that includes a reservoir, a pump driven by a motor or engine, and the valves and accessories needed to supply pressurised oil to actuators. Some people use "power pack" for smaller compact units and "power unit" or "HPU" for larger industrial systems, but there is no strict distinction.
### Can one power pack run several machines?
Yes, if it is designed for the combined flow and pressure demand and the circuits are arranged properly with suitable valves. Centralised systems can be efficient, but a failure then affects every connected machine, and long pipe runs add pressure losses. For critical machines, separate units or a central system with redundancy may be more appropriate.
### How noisy are hydraulic power packs?
Noise depends on pump type, motor speed, mounting and pressure. Gear pumps are typically louder than vane or internal gear pumps. Noise can be reduced by choosing quieter pump types, using lower motor speeds, isolating vibration with proper mounts and hoses, and fitting acoustic enclosures. Specify noise limits at the enquiry stage if the unit will be near operators.
### What maintenance does a power pack need?
Routine tasks include checking oil level and temperature, inspecting for leaks, changing filter elements based on indicators or analysis, cleaning coolers, checking pressures and monitoring pump noise. Periodic oil analysis on larger or critical units helps catch contamination and wear early. A short, consistent maintenance routine prevents most power pack failures.

## how-to-size-a-hydraulic-power-unit
### What happens if a power unit is undersized?
An undersized unit cannot deliver the required flow or pressure, so actuators move too slowly or cannot reach full force. The pump may run at its limits continuously, the relief valve may open frequently and the oil overheats. Components wear faster and the machine underperforms. Correct sizing based on the actual duty cycle avoids these problems.
### Is it better to oversize a power unit to be safe?
Moderate margins are sensible, but significant oversizing wastes energy, increases purchase cost and can cause heat problems if excess flow is dumped over the relief valve. A better approach is accurate sizing with a reasonable allowance for losses and future needs, using variable pumps or accumulators where demand varies.
### How does an accumulator reduce power unit size?
If a machine needs high flow only briefly, an accumulator can store oil during low-demand periods and release it during peaks. The pump and motor can then be sized for average demand rather than peak demand. This reduces motor size, energy use and heat. Accumulators must be correctly sized and fitted with appropriate safety blocks.
### What information do I need to give a supplier to size a unit?
Provide each actuator's force or torque, speed, stroke and timing, which functions operate simultaneously, the duty cycle, available power supply, control requirements, ambient conditions and space constraints. Drawings of the machine and a description of the cycle help the supplier propose an efficient design rather than a generic unit.

## hydraulic-fluid-contamination
### What does ISO 4406 cleanliness code mean?
It is a standard way of reporting how many particles of certain sizes are present in a fluid sample. The code consists of numbers representing particle counts in size ranges; each step up roughly doubles the particle count. Component manufacturers often specify target codes, and sensitive components require cleaner oil. Oil analysis laboratories report results using this code.
### How often should I take oil samples?
It depends on the criticality and size of the system. Critical or high-value systems may be sampled monthly or quarterly, while smaller systems may be sampled less often. Sample consistently from the same point while the system is running, using clean equipment, so results can be compared over time. Trends are more important than single readings.
### Is new hydraulic oil clean enough to use directly?
Often not. New oil can contain particles from manufacturing, storage and transfer that exceed the cleanliness levels recommended for hydraulic components. Filtering new oil through a transfer cart when filling or topping up is a simple way to avoid introducing contamination into the system.
### How do I know if water is in my hydraulic oil?
Water can make oil look cloudy or milky, though small amounts may be invisible. Oil analysis can measure water content accurately. Signs of water problems include corrosion, sludge, filter blocking and reduced component life. Sources include condensation, leaking coolers, washing equipment and poor storage, so find and fix the source as well as removing the water.

## choosing-hydraulic-oil
### Can I mix different brands of hydraulic oil?
It is best to avoid mixing. Oils with different additive chemistries can react, causing sludge, foaming or reduced performance. If mixing is unavoidable, check compatibility with the oil suppliers first. Clear labelling of oil types at storage areas and top-up points helps prevent accidental mixing.
### Which viscosity grade should I use?
Follow the equipment or pump manufacturer's recommendation, which is based on keeping the oil within an acceptable viscosity range at operating temperature and on cold starts. Common industrial grades include ISO VG 32, 46 and 68. Outdoor equipment with large temperature swings may benefit from a high viscosity index oil.
### How often should hydraulic oil be changed?
Ideally based on condition rather than a fixed interval. Oil analysis shows whether the oil is still fit for use by checking contamination, water, oxidation and additive levels. Where analysis is not available, follow the manufacturer's recommended interval and shorten it in harsh or high-temperature conditions.
### When should fire-resistant fluids be used?
Fire-resistant fluids are used where a hydraulic leak could reach an ignition source — near furnaces, molten metal, hot surfaces or in underground mining. Switching to a fire-resistant fluid requires checking compatibility of seals, hoses, filters and other materials, and usually requires thorough flushing. A risk assessment should guide the decision.

## hydraulic-system-overheating
### What is a normal operating temperature for hydraulic oil?
It depends on the oil, components and application, but many systems are designed to run within a moderate range well below the temperature at which seals and oil degrade rapidly. Check the oil and component manufacturers' recommendations. More important than a single number is consistency: a system that runs hotter than it used to is telling you something has changed.
### Can a larger cooler fix overheating?
It can control the symptom, but it does not fix wasted energy. If the system is overheating because oil continuously flows over a relief valve or through worn components, a bigger cooler only removes heat that should not be generated in the first place. Find and fix the source of heat first, then size cooling appropriately.
### Why does my system overheat only in summer?
Higher ambient temperatures reduce the temperature difference available for cooling, so the same system sheds less heat. Dust on cooler fins, poor ventilation and direct sunlight make it worse. Clean coolers, improve airflow and check that the system is not wasting energy. If necessary, add cooling capacity for peak conditions.
### How do I find where heat is being generated?
An infrared thermometer or thermal camera helps identify hot spots. A valve, motor or pump significantly hotter than nearby components is often where energy is being lost — for example, through internal leakage or continuous relief valve flow. Combining temperature readings with pressure measurements and circuit diagrams usually reveals the cause.

## hydraulic-accumulators-guide
### How often should accumulator pre-charge be checked?
Follow the manufacturer's recommendation, which depends on accumulator type and duty. Many maintenance programmes check pre-charge periodically and after any maintenance on the circuit. Checks must be done with the oil side fully depressurised and using proper charging and gauging equipment. Local regulations may also set inspection requirements for pressure vessels.
### Why must accumulators only be charged with nitrogen?
Nitrogen is inert and dry. Oxygen or compressed air mixed with hydraulic oil under pressure can create an explosion risk, and air introduces moisture. Always use dry nitrogen and the correct charging equipment, following the accumulator manufacturer's instructions.
### Can an accumulator be dangerous when the machine is off?
Yes. An accumulator can hold pressurised oil after the pump stops, which can move actuators unexpectedly or spray high-pressure oil when a fitting is loosened. Always discharge the oil side and verify zero pressure with a gauge before working on the circuit, and label systems containing accumulators.
### What are signs of a failed accumulator?
Signs include the pump cycling on and off more often, loss of emergency functions, more pressure pulsation or shock, and pressure dropping quickly when the pump stops. These may indicate lost pre-charge, a ruptured bladder or worn piston seals. Investigate promptly, as the accumulator may no longer provide the protection or performance the system relies on.

## reading-hydraulic-circuit-diagrams
### What standard do hydraulic symbols follow?
Most modern hydraulic schematics use symbols based on ISO 1219. Older drawings or those from different regions may use slightly different conventions, but the core ideas — circles for pumps and motors, boxes for valve positions, diamonds for filters and coolers — are widely recognised. A legend on the drawing helps interpret any special symbols.
### What do P, T, A and B mean on a valve?
P is the pressure port connected to the pump supply, T is the tank or return port, and A and B are the work ports connected to the actuator. Some valves also have drain (L or Y) and pilot (X) ports. Understanding these labels helps you trace where oil flows in each valve position.
### Why is the valve drawn in a particular position?
Valves are drawn in their normal (unactuated) position — the position they take when no signal or force is applied. This is the box connected to the circuit lines. To understand other positions, imagine sliding the boxes across so that a different box connects to the lines.
### My machine has no circuit diagram. What should I do?
Ask the equipment manufacturer first. If no diagram is available, a hydraulic engineer can survey the system and create one. A current, accurate schematic makes troubleshooting faster, supports safe maintenance and helps when ordering replacement components or planning upgrades.

## hydraulic-power-unit-maintenance-checklist
### Who should carry out daily checks on a power unit?
Operators are usually best placed to perform simple daily checks: oil level, temperature, visible leaks, unusual noise and filter indicators. They see the machine every day and notice changes. Detailed inspections, adjustments and repairs should be performed by trained maintenance personnel following safe isolation procedures.
### Should filters be changed on a schedule or by indicator?
Ideally by condition — when the clogging indicator approaches its limit or oil analysis shows cleanliness drifting from target — with a maximum time interval as a safety net. Changing purely by calendar can waste elements or, worse, leave a blocked filter in bypass for weeks. Always investigate sudden increases in filter change frequency.
### Why keep maintenance logs?
Logs reveal trends that single inspections miss: gradual temperature increases, rising case drain flow, more frequent filter changes or longer cycle times. These trends often give months of warning before a failure, allowing repairs to be planned around production rather than forced by breakdowns.
### Which spares should be kept for a power unit?
At minimum, filter elements, seal kits for key components and common hoses. For critical units, add a spare solenoid coil, pressure gauges and, where downtime would be very costly, a spare pump or complete motor-pump assembly. Review spares after each breakdown to see what would have shortened the repair.

## hydraulic-filtration-explained
### Should I fit a fine filter on the pump suction?
Usually not. Fine filters on the suction side create pressure drop that can starve the pump and cause cavitation, especially with cold oil. Suction strainers are normally coarse to protect against large debris, while fine filtration is placed in the return line, pressure line or an offline loop. Follow the pump manufacturer's inlet requirements.
### What is a beta ratio?
The beta ratio describes filter efficiency at a specific particle size: the number of particles upstream divided by the number downstream. A higher ratio means the filter captures a higher proportion of particles of that size. Comparing beta ratios at the same particle size is a better way to compare filters than comparing nominal micron ratings.
### What happens when a filter goes into bypass?
When the element becomes clogged, a bypass valve opens to protect the filter and maintain flow. Unfiltered oil then circulates through the system, allowing contamination to reach sensitive components. Clogging indicators warn you before bypass occurs, so elements can be changed in time.
### Is offline filtration worth adding?
For large reservoirs, critical systems or systems that struggle to reach target cleanliness, offline (kidney loop) filtration is very effective. It filters continuously, independent of machine operation, and can also remove water with suitable elements. It is often one of the most cost-effective ways to improve component life.
