Truck-mounted, all-terrain, rough-terrain and crawler cranes depend on hydraulics for almost every motion: setting up outriggers, raising and extending the boom, hoisting loads and slewing. Because these cranes lift heavy loads over people and property, their hydraulic systems are safety-critical and must be maintained with care.

## Main hydraulic functions

### Outriggers

Hydraulic cylinders extend beams and jacks to level and stabilise the crane. Each jack cylinder carries a large share of the crane's weight and load, so load-holding valves are fitted to prevent collapse if a hose fails.

### Boom luffing

Large luffing (derricking) cylinders raise and lower the boom. They operate at high pressures and are equipped with counterbalance valves to control lowering speed and hold the boom safely.

### Boom telescoping

Telescopic booms are extended using internal cylinders and, in many designs, rope or locking systems. Telescoping cylinders may be very long and require careful design against buckling.

### Hoisting

Hydraulic motors drive winches through gearboxes, with spring-applied brakes and counterbalance or brake valves controlling the load.

### Slewing

Hydraulic motors and gearboxes rotate the superstructure, with brakes holding position.

### Steering and suspension

Many mobile cranes also use hydraulics for steering and suspension.

## Safety-critical components

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| Counterbalance valves | Control lowering and hold loads on cylinders and winches |
| Pilot-operated check valves | Hold outrigger jacks and cylinders if hoses fail |
| Winch brakes | Hold suspended loads |
| Relief valves | Limit system pressure |
| Load moment indicator sensors | Provide data for safe operation, often including hydraulic pressure transducers |

These components must be maintained and adjusted only by competent personnel according to the manufacturer's instructions.

## Common problems

- **Boom drift** from cylinder internal leakage or valve faults.
- **Outrigger jacks creeping** from check valve or seal problems.
- **Jerky telescoping** from air, contamination or seal friction.
- **Hydraulic leaks** from hoses, fittings and cylinder seals.
- **Overheating** during long working periods.

## Maintenance priorities

1. **Daily checks:** leaks, hose damage, oil level, outrigger and boom function.
2. **Rod inspection:** luffing and outrigger cylinder rods are exposed and easily damaged.
3. **Filter changes** and oil analysis.
4. **Hose replacement** based on condition and age.
5. **Valve testing** for load-holding devices during periodic inspections.
6. **Cylinder overhauls** at intervals or when drift exceeds acceptable limits.
7. **Statutory inspections** as required by local lifting equipment regulations.

## Repairing crane cylinders

Mobile crane cylinders are high-value, safety-critical components. Repairs should include full inspection, rod and barrel repair as required, correct seal kits, and pressure and function testing with documented results. Load-holding valves should be checked or replaced as part of the repair.

## Working practices

- Always set up on firm, level ground with outriggers fully deployed as required by the load chart.
- Never exceed rated capacities.
- Avoid shock loading and sudden control movements.
- Report any drift, leaks or unusual behaviour immediately.

Many industrial machines are built to last decades. Their frames, structures and mechanical components may still be in good condition, while their hydraulic systems have become unreliable, inefficient or impossible to maintain due to obsolete parts. Retrofitting modern hydraulics can be far cheaper than replacing the whole machine.

## Signs a retrofit makes sense

- Frequent hydraulic breakdowns and leaks.
- Obsolete pumps, valves or controls with no available spares.
- High energy consumption and overheating.
- Poor control accuracy affecting product quality.
- Safety systems that do not meet current expectations.
- Increased production demands the existing system cannot meet.

## What a retrofit can include

### New power unit

A modern power pack with efficient pumps, proper filtration, cooling and monitoring. Variable displacement pumps or variable speed drives can significantly reduce energy use.

### Updated valves and controls

Replacing manual or old solenoid valves with modern directional, proportional or servo valves improves control, repeatability and diagnostics. Integration with PLC controls allows recipes, monitoring and fault reporting.

### Cylinder upgrades

Cylinders can be rebuilt with modern seals, upgraded rods or redesigned for new loads. Position sensors can be integrated for closed-loop control.

### Piping and hoses

Replacing leaking threaded pipework with properly designed tubing, flanges and modern fittings reduces leaks and contamination.

### Safety improvements

Load-holding valves, safety valves, monitored valves and pressure monitoring improve safety, along with guarding and control upgrades as appropriate.

## Benefits

| Benefit | How it is achieved |
|---|---|
| Energy savings | Variable pumps, VFDs, accumulators, efficient circuits |
| Reliability | New components, better filtration and cooling |
| Productivity | Faster, more consistent cycles |
| Quality | Precise control of force, speed and position |
| Maintainability | Standard, available components and documentation |
| Safety | Modern safety valves and monitoring |

## The retrofit process

1. **Survey:** document the existing system, measure performance and identify problems.
2. **Requirements:** define what the machine must achieve now and in future.
3. **Design:** create a new circuit, select components and plan integration.
4. **Manufacture and test:** build and test the power unit and assemblies before installation.
5. **Installation:** plan installation during a shutdown to minimise downtime.
6. **Commissioning:** flush, set up and verify performance.
7. **Documentation and training:** provide circuit diagrams, parts lists and operator training.

## Minimising downtime

Pre-building and testing the new power unit off-site, preparing hoses and pipework in advance and planning the installation carefully allows many retrofits to be completed in a short shutdown.

## Is retrofit always the answer?

If the machine's structure is worn out or the process has fundamentally changed, a new machine may be better. A survey helps make that decision with facts rather than assumptions.

Tipper trucks and dump trailers rely on hydraulic cylinders — usually telescopic — to raise their bodies and discharge loads. These cylinders work in dusty, muddy conditions with heavy and sometimes uneven loads. They are also involved in some of the most serious accidents in road haulage when tipping goes wrong.

## How a tipper hydraulic system works

1. A **power take-off (PTO)** from the truck gearbox drives a hydraulic pump.
2. A **tipping valve** directs oil to the cylinder to raise the body, holds it, and allows oil to return to lower it.
3. A **telescopic cylinder** — usually single-acting — extends in stages to raise the body. Gravity lowers it.
4. **Safety features** include relief valves, stroke limiters and, on many systems, controls that prevent overextension.

## Common problems

| Symptom | Possible causes |
|---|---|
| Body will not raise fully | Low oil, pump wear, relief setting, internal leakage |
| Body raises slowly | PTO or pump problems, low engine speed, restriction |
| Body drops or creeps when raised | Valve or seal leakage |
| Jerky or uneven staging | Air, contamination, worn stage bearings |
| External leaks between stages | Worn or damaged stage seals, scored stages |
| Bent cylinder stages | Side loading from tipping on uneven ground or sticky loads |

## Safe tipping practices

Many tipper accidents — including overturns — are caused by unsafe tipping conditions rather than equipment faults. Safe practice includes:

- **Tip only on firm, level ground.**
- **Check for overhead hazards** such as power lines before raising the body.
- **Ensure loads discharge freely;** sticky loads such as wet clay or asphalt can hold weight high in the body and destabilise the vehicle.
- **Keep the tailgate open** and properly secured before tipping.
- **Never drive with the body raised.**
- **Never work under a raised body** without a suitable mechanical prop correctly in place.
- **Keep people clear** during tipping.

## Maintenance checklist

- Check **oil level** with the body lowered.
- Inspect the **cylinder stages** for damage, scoring and leaks when extended for checks.
- Clean **mud and debris** from stages and mountings.
- **Grease** mounting pivots and trunnions.
- Inspect **hoses and fittings** for damage and leaks.
- Check the **PTO and pump** mountings and operation.
- Change **filters and oil** at recommended intervals.
- Test **safety devices** such as stroke limiters and alarms.

## Repairing telescopic tipper cylinders

Reconditioning typically includes resealing all stages, replacing stage bearings and stop rings, repairing or re-plating damaged stages and pressure testing. Bent stages usually need replacement rather than straightening.

When ordering repairs or replacements, provide:

- number of stages;
- retracted and extended lengths;
- largest stage diameter;
- mounting types;
- working pressure;
- body capacity and typical loads.

## Prevent repeat failures

If cylinders bend or fail repeatedly, look at tipping practices, loading, ground conditions and whether the cylinder size suits the body and load. Upgrading to a heavier cylinder may be appropriate for demanding applications.
