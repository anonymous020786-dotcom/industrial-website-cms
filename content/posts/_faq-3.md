# FAQ add-ons — crane wheels & drives, manufacturing & engineering

## crane-wheel-wear-causes
### How do I measure crane wheel flange wear?
Use a flange wear gauge or calipers to measure flange thickness at a consistent height on the flange, and compare it with the minimum value given by the crane or wheel manufacturer. Measure every wheel and record results so you can compare wear rates over time. Measurements must be taken with the crane isolated and with safe access to the wheels.
### Is some flange wear normal?
A small amount of flange contact is normal, particularly when the crane starts, stops or travels over rail joints. Heavy, rapid or one-sided flange wear is not normal and usually points to misalignment, skewing, runway problems or unequal wheel diameters. Investigate the cause before replacing the wheels.
### Why do crane wheels get flat spots?
Flat spots form when a wheel slides along the rail instead of rolling. Typical causes include brakes that lock the wheels, sudden stops, drive problems or seized bearings. Flat spots cause impact loads and noise every revolution, so worn wheels should be replaced and the cause corrected.
### Can crane wheels be re-machined?
In some cases, worn treads can be re-machined if enough material and hardened depth remain and the wheel design allows. However, re-machining changes the diameter, which matters for driven wheels that must be matched. Surface-hardened wheels may lose their hardened layer. Often replacement is the more reliable solution.
### How long should crane wheels last?
Wheel life depends on load, duty class, wheel material and hardness, rail condition and alignment. In good conditions wheels can last many years; on misaligned cranes they may wear out in months. Tracking wear rates is the best way to predict replacement and detect problems.

## single-vs-double-flange-crane-wheels
### What is flange clearance and why does it matter?
Flange clearance is the space between the wheel flanges and the rail head. It allows for runway tolerances and thermal movement. Too little clearance causes binding and rapid wear; too much allows excessive skewing before correction. The correct clearance is set by the crane design.
### Can I convert double-flange wheels to flangeless with guide rollers?
It is possible on some cranes, but it is a design change that affects how lateral forces are transferred to the structure. It should be engineered by qualified crane specialists, considering the end carriage design, runway and guide roller mounting.
### Why do some cranes use tapered treads?
Tapered treads, used with appropriate rail heads and drive arrangements, can help the crane self-centre and reduce skewing. They must be used as designed; mixing tapered and cylindrical treads incorrectly can create tracking problems.
### Are single-flange wheels less safe?
Not when used correctly. Single-flange wheels are used where the design provides guidance on one side, such as a guide rail. Safety depends on the overall design, installation and maintenance rather than the number of flanges alone.
### What information do I need to order replacement wheels?
Provide the tread diameter, tread width, flange type and dimensions, rail type, bore and axle or bearing details, hardness requirements, crane capacity and duty, and drawings or photos of the existing wheel. Mention any wear problems so improvements can be considered.

## crane-wheel-materials-heat-treatment
### Are forged wheels better than cast wheels?
Forged wheels generally have a more refined grain structure and fewer internal defects, making them a common choice for heavy-duty cranes. High-quality cast wheels can perform well, especially for large sizes, provided casting quality is controlled. The best choice depends on size, duty and cost.
### What hardness should crane wheels have?
There is no single answer. Hardness should suit the wheel load, duty and rail material. Very hard wheels can accelerate rail wear, while soft wheels wear quickly. Wheel manufacturers and crane designers select hardness to balance wheel and rail life.
### What does case depth mean for induction-hardened wheels?
Case depth is the depth of the hardened layer below the surface. It must be deep enough to support contact stresses and to allow for normal wear over the wheel's life. If the case is too shallow, the hardened layer can wear through or crack.
### How can I verify wheel hardness?
Request a hardness test report from the manufacturer. For critical wheels, you can also specify testing of sample parts or sections to confirm hardened depth. Portable hardness testers can check surface hardness on site.
### Why did my new wheels crack?
Cracking can result from excessive hardness with insufficient toughness, poor heat treatment, material defects, sharp design features or severe impact loads. A failure investigation, including material and hardness checks, helps identify the cause.

## crane-skewing-rail-alignment
### How do I know if my crane is skewing?
Common signs include heavy flange wear on diagonally opposite wheels, wear marks on the side of the rail heads, squealing during travel and visible crabbing of the bridge. Measuring wheel diameters and checking drive performance helps confirm the cause.
### What is a runway survey?
A runway survey measures rail straightness, span (gauge), elevation and joint condition along the full length of the runway. It provides objective data to identify misalignment and plan corrections.
### Can variable frequency drives reduce skewing?
Yes. VFDs provide smooth acceleration and deceleration and can balance drives more effectively than older control systems, reducing skewing forces. They work best combined with proper alignment and matched wheels.
### Should I replace wheels before fixing alignment?
No. Fix alignment, runway and drive issues first, then fit new wheels. Otherwise the new wheels will wear quickly in the same way as the old ones.
### Can building settlement affect crane rails?
Yes. Settlement, thermal movement and structural changes can shift runway beams and rails over time. Periodic surveys, especially after building work, help detect these changes.

## crane-end-carriage-assemblies
### What is the difference between an end carriage and an end truck?
They are the same component. Both terms describe the assembly at each end of the bridge that carries the wheels and connects to the girders. Terminology varies by region and manufacturer.
### When should end carriages be replaced rather than repaired?
Consider replacement when frames are bent or cracked, bearing housings are badly worn, multiple components are near end of life, or when upgrading drives and wheels. Replacement can also reduce downtime if the new assembly is prepared in advance.
### Can I upgrade end carriages on an old crane?
Yes, many older cranes are upgraded with modern end carriages, wheel blocks and drives. The upgrade must suit the crane's structure, span, capacity and runway, and should be engineered by qualified crane specialists.
### What are rail sweepers for?
Rail sweepers clear debris from the rail ahead of the wheels, preventing wheel damage and derailment risk. They should be present, correctly adjusted and in good condition.
### Why do bearing housings wear?
Wear can result from fretting, incorrect fits, contamination, overload and impacts. Worn housings allow bearings and wheels to move out of alignment, accelerating wheel wear.

## crane-wheel-bearings-maintenance
### How often should crane wheel bearings be greased?
Follow the bearing or crane manufacturer's recommended interval, then adjust it for your duty and environment. Cranes running multiple shifts, in hot areas such as steel plants, or in dusty and wet conditions usually need more frequent lubrication than a lightly used workshop crane. Use the specified grease, a clean grease gun and the correct quantity — over-greasing pushes out seals and causes bearings to run hot, while under-greasing leads to wear. Recording each lubrication task makes it easy to confirm that critical cranes are not missed.
### Can I mix different types of grease?
Avoid it unless compatibility has been confirmed. Greases use different thickeners and base oils, and incompatible greases can soften and run out of the bearing or harden into a paste that no longer lubricates. If you need to change grease type, purge the old grease thoroughly according to the manufacturer's guidance and label lubrication points clearly so the same product is used every time.
### How can I detect bearing problems before they fail?
Listen for rumbling, grinding or clicking as the crane travels, and check wheel blocks with an infrared thermometer or thermal camera — a bearing running noticeably hotter than its neighbours is a warning sign. During inspections, check for wheel wobble, axial play, leaking or discoloured grease and damaged seals. Catching these signs early allows bearings to be replaced during planned maintenance rather than after a seizure damages the axle or housing.
### Why do newly replaced bearings sometimes fail quickly?
The most common causes are installation damage from hammering, worn or incorrectly sized shaft and housing seats, dirt introduced during assembly, the wrong grease or quantity, and misalignment from worn housings or bent axles. Using induction heaters and proper pullers, checking fits, working in clean conditions and replacing seals with every bearing change prevent most early failures. Pre-assembled wheel blocks built in a workshop avoid many of these risks.
### Are sealed bearings better for outdoor cranes?
For outdoor and dusty applications, better sealing usually pays off. Water, dust and scale are the main enemies of crane wheel bearings, and effective seals keep grease in and contamination out. The best arrangement depends on speed, temperature and maintenance access, so describe the environment when ordering wheels or assemblies and ask the supplier to recommend suitable sealing.

## crane-rail-wear-effects
### How do I know when crane rails need replacing?
Look for severe head wear, significant side wear from flange contact, cracks, damaged or stepped joints that cannot be repaired, and loose or broken fixings that keep recurring. A runway inspection or survey shows whether problems are local — fixable with re-fastening, shimming or joint repairs — or widespread enough to justify replacement. Because rail replacement is disruptive, plan it together with wheel replacement and any runway beam repairs.
### Can worn rails damage brand-new wheels?
Yes. Uneven rail heads concentrate contact stresses on part of the tread, stepped joints create impacts, and misaligned rails push flanges into the rail sides. New wheels on a poor runway can wear out much faster than the wheels they replaced. Inspect and correct rail problems before or at the same time as fitting new wheels.
### What do rail clips do and why do they fail?
Rail clips hold the rail to the runway beam while allowing controlled thermal movement. They fail through vibration, corrosion, impact and incorrect installation. Loose or missing clips allow rails to move sideways and rock under load, causing misalignment, noise and wear. Check clips during routine crane inspections and replace damaged ones promptly.
### Should crane wheels be harder than the rails?
Wheel and rail hardness should be balanced so neither wears excessively. If wheels are much harder than the rails, the rails wear faster — and rails are usually more expensive and disruptive to replace. If wheels are much softer, they wear quickly. Tell your wheel supplier the rail type and condition so they can recommend suitable hardness.
### How often should crane runways be surveyed?
Many sites survey runways when wheel wear becomes excessive, after building modifications or settlement, after crane collisions, and at intervals for high-duty cranes. A baseline survey when a crane is new or after major repairs makes later comparisons much more useful.

## replacing-crane-wheels-matched-pairs
### What does "matched pair" mean for crane wheels?
It means driven wheels manufactured and measured so their tread diameters are within a tight tolerance of each other. Because each revolution moves the crane a distance equal to the wheel's circumference, matched diameters ensure both sides of the bridge travel the same distance. Ask your supplier to record the measured diameter of each wheel on the inspection report so you can verify the match.
### Can I replace idler wheels one at a time?
Often yes, because idler wheels do not drive the crane and small diameter differences have less effect on tracking. However, a single worn idler can indicate a wider problem such as misalignment or a damaged end carriage, so check the overall wear pattern before replacing it in isolation.
### How close do driven wheel diameters need to be?
The acceptable difference depends on the crane design, span, speed and drive arrangement, so it should come from the crane manufacturer or an experienced wheel supplier. As a rule, the tighter the match the better, especially on common drives where both wheels turn at exactly the same speed.
### Why do diameter differences matter more on common drives?
With a common (line-shaft) drive, both driven wheels rotate at identical speed. Any diameter difference means one side covers more distance every revolution, so the crane steadily skews until flanges force it back. On separate drives the effect still exists but can be partly offset by the drive control.
### How can I plan wheel replacements to avoid breakdowns?
Measure tread diameters and flange thickness at regular inspections and record them. Plotting wear over time shows when wheels will reach their limits, giving enough notice to order a matched set and replace it during a planned shutdown rather than after a failure.

## crane-travel-drives-gearboxes-couplings
### How often should crane gearbox oil be changed?
Follow the gearbox manufacturer's interval, which is typically based on operating hours and oil type, and shorten it for hot, dusty or heavy-duty conditions. Oil analysis can extend or shorten intervals based on actual condition and also reveals wear metals that indicate developing bearing or gear damage. Always check oil levels during routine inspections, because low oil is a common cause of gearbox failures.
### What causes a clunk when the crane starts or stops?
Typical causes are worn coupling elements, excessive gear backlash, loose gearbox mounting bolts or worn torque arms. These problems create shock loads every time the crane accelerates or brakes, which accelerates wear on gears and bearings and can loosen structural connections. Inspect and replace worn parts promptly.
### Is it worth upgrading an older crane to VFD control?
On busy cranes it often is. Variable frequency drives give smooth acceleration and deceleration, reduce load swing, improve positioning and reduce mechanical shock on gearboxes, couplings, wheels and structures. The upgrade must be engineered to suit the existing motors, brakes and control system, and may require motor or brake changes on very old cranes.
### Why do travel motors overheat?
Frequent inching, brakes that drag or release late, overloading, incorrect control settings and high ambient temperatures are common causes. Replacing an overheated motor without finding the cause usually leads to a repeat failure, so check brakes, operating practice and drive settings first.
### Which drive spare parts are worth keeping?
Coupling elements, brake linings and coils, gearbox seals and contactors are inexpensive and commonly needed. For cranes that would stop production if a drive failed, a complete spare geared motor or gearbox can turn a multi-day breakdown into a few hours of work.

## cnc-machining-hydraulic-components
### Why do hydraulic parts need such tight tolerances?
Seals are designed for specific groove dimensions and clearances. A groove that is slightly too deep or wide lets the seal move and leak; one that is too shallow over-compresses it, increasing friction and wear. Clearances behind seals control extrusion at high pressure. Concentricity between pistons, rods and glands keeps seals evenly loaded. Small dimensional errors therefore have large effects on reliability.
### Can CNC machining reproduce old or obsolete parts?
Yes. Once a worn part has been measured and an approved drawing produced, CNC machining can reproduce it accurately, and the program can be stored so future orders match exactly. This makes CNC machining central to reverse engineering and to keeping older machines running.
### What surface finish do sealing surfaces need?
Seal manufacturers specify roughness ranges for dynamic and static sealing surfaces. Too rough a surface wears seals like sandpaper; too smooth may not hold an adequate oil film. Finishing processes such as grinding, honing, polishing or roller burnishing are used to achieve the specified finish, which should be shown on the drawing.
### Why is deburring so important for hydraulic parts?
Burrs and sharp edges on ports, cross-drillings and grooves cut seals during assembly and can break off later as metal contamination inside the system. Thorough deburring, edge-breaking and cleaning after machining protect both the seals and every downstream component.
### What inspection should machined parts receive?
At minimum, critical dimensions, threads and sealing surface finishes should be checked and recorded. First-article inspection confirms a new program is correct, and periodic checks during batch production ensure consistency. For customer-critical parts, request a dimensional inspection report.

## honing-hydraulic-cylinder-tubes
### What is the purpose of the cross-hatch pattern?
The fine cross-hatch left by honing stones creates a controlled surface texture that holds a thin film of oil. That film lubricates the piston seals and wear rings, reducing friction and wear while still allowing the seals to seal effectively. The angle and depth of the pattern are controlled by the honing process.
### How much material does re-honing remove?
Only as much as needed to remove defects and restore geometry, but every hone enlarges the bore slightly. Before honing, the repairer measures the bore and wall thickness to confirm it will remain within the seal manufacturer's tolerance and the pressure rating. If defects are too deep, the tube must be replaced or an oversize solution engineered.
### Can a bulged cylinder barrel be repaired?
No. A bulged barrel has been permanently deformed by overpressure or shock and the material has been overstressed. Honing would remove the visible bulge but not the damage, so bulged barrels should always be replaced and the cause of the overpressure investigated.
### Why must honing residue be removed completely?
Honing produces fine abrasive and metal particles. If any remain in the barrel, they act as grinding paste on new seals and spread through the hydraulic system. Thorough washing, flushing and inspection after honing are essential parts of a quality cylinder repair.
### What is the difference between honing and skiving and roller burnishing?
Skiving and roller burnishing cut the bore to size and then roll it smooth, producing an excellent finish quickly — a common method for new cylinder tubes. Honing uses abrasive stones to correct geometry and create a controlled finish, and is particularly useful for repairs and for correcting ovality or taper.

## induction-hardening-explained
### Which steels can be induction hardened?
Medium-carbon steels and suitable alloy steels respond well because they contain enough carbon to form a hard structure when quenched. Low-carbon structural steels do not harden significantly. Material selection and heat treatment should therefore be specified together, and the drawing should state the required hardness and case depth.
### Does induction hardening cause distortion?
It can cause some distortion, although usually less than through-hardening a large part because only the surface is heated. Long shafts and thin sections are most affected. Manufacturers often leave a small allowance for straightening or finish grinding after hardening to achieve final dimensions.
### How is hardened case depth checked?
Case depth is usually verified by hardness testing across a sectioned sample or test piece made with the same process parameters. Surface hardness can be checked on every part with portable testers. Drawings should define how case depth is measured so results are consistent.
### Can induction-hardened areas be welded?
Welding on or near hardened zones can create brittle structures and cracks. If a part must be welded — for example, a rod end — the drawing should specify which areas remain soft. Welding procedures for hardened parts must be carefully controlled and are best avoided where possible.
### Why is tempering done after hardening?
Freshly quenched steel is very hard but can be brittle and highly stressed. Tempering at a lower temperature relieves some of these stresses and improves toughness, reducing the risk of cracking in service while retaining most of the hardness.

## welding-quality-heavy-fabrication
### What is a welding procedure specification (WPS)?
A WPS documents exactly how a weld should be made: process, base and filler materials, joint preparation, preheat, welding parameters and technique. Procedures are qualified by welding and testing samples, proving that welds made this way achieve the required strength and toughness. Welders follow the WPS so results are consistent.
### Why is preheating sometimes required?
Thick sections and higher-strength steels cool quickly after welding, which can trap hydrogen and form hard, brittle structures that crack. Preheating slows cooling and reduces this risk. The required preheat depends on material, thickness and welding process, and is specified in the welding procedure.
### Which NDT methods are used on welds?
Visual inspection is always the first step. Dye penetrant and magnetic particle testing find surface and near-surface cracks, while ultrasonic and radiographic testing detect internal defects such as lack of fusion and inclusions. The methods and extent of testing should match the criticality of the component and any applicable standard.
### Can all weld defects be found visually?
No. Visual inspection finds surface problems such as undercut, surface porosity and obvious cracks, but internal defects require volumetric methods like ultrasonic or radiographic testing. For critical load-bearing or pressure-containing welds, specify appropriate NDT.
### Should I ask suppliers for welding records?
For critical fabrications — lifting equipment, pressure components, structural parts — yes. Welding procedures, welder qualifications and NDT reports demonstrate that welding was controlled and provide traceability if questions arise later.

## pressure-testing-hydraulic-components
### What pressure should a component be tested at?
The test pressure must come from the design specification or applicable standard for that component, and must never exceed what the component and test equipment are designed to withstand. Proof tests are typically above normal working pressure, while function tests may be performed at working pressure. Ask your supplier what pressure they use and why.
### Why is trapped air dangerous during pressure tests?
Air is compressible and stores far more energy than oil at the same pressure. If a component fails while air is trapped inside, that energy is released violently, potentially ejecting parts or fittings. Bleeding air completely before pressurising and testing behind guards are basic safety requirements.
### What does an internal leakage test show?
By pressurising one side of a cylinder piston and monitoring the opposite port, the test shows whether oil is passing the piston seal. Excessive leakage indicates a damaged or incorrectly installed seal, a scored barrel or other internal faults that would cause drift or loss of force in service.
### Do repaired components need pressure testing?
Yes. A repair involves dismantling, machining and reassembly, any of which can introduce problems. Testing after repair confirms seals are correctly installed, surfaces are sealing and the component functions properly before it is fitted to a machine.
### What should a pressure test record include?
Component identification, drawing revision, test pressure and duration, test medium and temperature, internal and external leakage results, function check results, gauge identification and calibration reference, the technician's name and date, and a clear pass/fail statement.

## engineering-drawings-tolerances-fits
### What is a general tolerance?
A general tolerance applies to every dimension on the drawing that does not carry its own tolerance. It is usually stated in the title block, often by reference to a standard tolerance class. This avoids cluttering the drawing while still defining acceptable variation for non-critical features.
### What does a fit like H7/g6 mean?
It is a notation from the ISO system of limits and fits. The capital letter and number describe the hole's tolerance zone and the lowercase letter and number describe the shaft's. H7/g6, for example, gives a close clearance fit commonly used for precise sliding or rotating parts. Other combinations produce transition or interference fits.
### Why is the drawing revision so important?
Designs change over time to fix problems or improve parts. Manufacturing from an outdated revision can produce parts that do not fit, use the wrong material or repeat a known failure. Always check and state the current revision when ordering.
### What is geometric tolerancing?
Geometric dimensioning and tolerancing controls the form, orientation and location of features — for example straightness, roundness, perpendicularity, runout and position. These controls are essential for rotating and sealing parts where size alone does not guarantee function.
### What if I do not have a drawing for a part?
A manufacturer can reverse-engineer the part from a sample, produce a drawing for your approval and then manufacture it. Keep the approved drawing on file so future orders are fast and you are not dependent on a single source.

## material-selection-heavy-duty-components
### What is a material test certificate?
It is a document issued by the steel mill or supplier recording the chemical composition and mechanical properties of a specific batch of material. It provides traceability and evidence that the material meets the specified grade — important for load-bearing and safety-critical parts.
### Is stainless steel always the best choice for durability?
Not necessarily. Stainless steels resist corrosion, but many grades have lower strength or wear resistance than heat-treated alloy steels and can be more expensive and harder to machine. They are the right choice where corrosion is the dominant problem; elsewhere, alloy steels with suitable coatings may perform better.
### Why does heat treatment matter as much as the steel grade?
The same steel can be soft and tough, or hard and wear-resistant, depending on how it is heat-treated. A part made from the correct steel but with the wrong heat treatment can wear quickly or crack. Always specify heat treatment and target hardness together with the material.
### What materials are used for bushes and bearings?
Bronze is common for sliding bushes because it works well against steel with lubrication. Hardened steel bushes suit high loads and abrasive conditions, while engineering plastics offer low friction and corrosion resistance. The choice depends on load, speed, lubrication and environment.
### Can I upgrade materials when ordering replacements?
Yes, and it is often worthwhile when the original part wore or failed early. However, changes to load-bearing components should be reviewed by a qualified engineer to make sure the new material and heat treatment are suitable for the loads and do not create new problems.

## surface-treatments-corrosion-protection
### Is hot-dip galvanising better than painting?
Galvanising provides a thick, tough zinc coating that protects steel sacrificially even when scratched, making it excellent for structures, walkways and brackets outdoors. Paint systems offer colour choice and can be applied to assembled equipment and maintained on site. The best choice depends on the environment, component size, design and maintenance plans — and some structures use both (duplex systems).
### Do chrome-plated hydraulic rods corrode?
They can. Chrome plating contains microscopic cracks, and in salty, humid or chemical environments moisture can reach the steel beneath, causing pitting, especially on rods left extended. For harsh environments, consider upgraded coatings or stainless rods and protect rods during storage.
### What is galvanic corrosion and how is it prevented?
Galvanic corrosion occurs when two different metals are in electrical contact in the presence of moisture; the less noble metal corrodes faster. It is prevented by choosing compatible materials, isolating dissimilar metals with non-conductive washers or sleeves, and protecting joints from moisture.
### How often should protective coatings be inspected?
Inspect coatings during routine equipment inspections and more often in severe environments such as ports and chemical plants. Repair chips, scratches and failed areas promptly, because corrosion spreads under damaged coatings and becomes much more expensive to fix later.
### Do coatings affect hydraulic seals?
Coatings on dynamic sealing surfaces — such as piston rods — must be compatible with seal materials and achieve the correct surface finish. Not every corrosion-resistant coating is suitable for a sliding seal surface, so coating changes should be agreed with the cylinder manufacturer.

## custom-component-manufacturing-process
### How can I speed up a custom manufacturing project?
Provide complete information at the start — drawings or samples, operating conditions, quantities and documentation requirements — nominate a technical contact who can answer questions quickly, approve drawings promptly and agree to order long-lead materials or bought-in components early. These steps often save more time than any change in the workshop.
### Why do suppliers ask for drawing approval?
Approval confirms that the design matches your requirements before money and time are spent on manufacturing. It catches misunderstandings about dimensions, mountings, materials or interfaces, which are far cheaper to correct on paper than on finished parts.
### Is a prototype always required?
No. Exact replacements made to an existing approved drawing usually go straight into production. New designs, significant modifications and critical components benefit from a prototype or first article that can be inspected, tested and trialled before full production.
### What documentation should come with custom components?
Typically an approved drawing, dimensional inspection report and, depending on the component, test records, hardness results and material certificates. For complete hydraulic systems, circuit diagrams, parts lists and operating instructions should also be supplied. Agree requirements at the order stage.
### Are repeat orders faster?
Yes. With an approved drawing, proven CNC programs, fixtures and an inspection plan already in place, repeat orders skip engineering and approval and benefit from experience gained on the first batch.
