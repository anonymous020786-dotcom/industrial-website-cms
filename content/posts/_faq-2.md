# FAQ add-ons — pumps & motors, crane maintenance

## gear-vs-vane-vs-piston-pumps
### Which hydraulic pump type is the most efficient?
Piston pumps generally offer the highest volumetric and overall efficiency, especially at high pressures, and variable displacement versions can save significant energy by delivering only the flow the system needs. Vane pumps are efficient at medium pressures. Gear pumps are typically the least efficient, and their efficiency drops further as they wear. For machines that run long hours, the energy cost difference can outweigh the higher purchase price of a piston pump.
### Can I replace a gear pump with a piston pump?
Often yes, but it is not a simple swap. A piston pump may need a case drain line, different mounting and shaft details, cleaner oil and different control settings. The circuit may also need changes to take advantage of variable displacement. Have the change reviewed by a hydraulic engineer so the new pump is installed and set up correctly.
### Why are gear pumps so common on mobile equipment?
They are compact, robust, relatively tolerant of contamination and inexpensive. On many mobile machines, a gear pump provides adequate performance for auxiliary functions at a low cost, and replacement is quick and easy. More demanding mobile machines, such as excavators and cranes, often use piston pumps for their main functions.
### What causes a vane pump to fail early?
Common causes include contamination, which wears the cam ring and vanes; running with oil that is too thick at start-up, which can stop vanes extending properly; cavitation from inlet restrictions; and running at pressures above the pump's rating. Clean oil, correct viscosity and good inlet conditions are the keys to long vane pump life.
### How do I identify which pump I have?
Look for a nameplate or part number on the pump body. If it is missing, note the mounting flange, shaft type, port sizes, rotation direction and physical construction, and photograph the pump from several angles. A hydraulic specialist can usually identify the type and displacement from this information, or by inspecting the pump.

## hydraulic-pump-cavitation-aeration
### How can I tell cavitation from aeration by sound?
Cavitation usually produces a steady, high-pitched whine that changes with pump speed and inlet conditions. Aeration tends to sound more erratic — a knocking, rattling or "marbles" noise — often accompanied by foam in the reservoir. The sound alone is not conclusive, so check inlet vacuum, oil level, foam and suction fittings to confirm.
### Can cold weather cause cavitation?
Yes. Cold oil is much thicker, so the pump has to work harder to draw it through the suction line. The inlet pressure can drop below the pump's limit, causing cavitation. Warming the oil before heavy operation, using an oil grade suited to the climate and ensuring the suction line is generously sized all help.
### Is it safe to look for suction leaks with the pump running?
Only with great care and following safe procedures for working near rotating machinery. A common method is applying clean oil around suction fittings and listening for a change in pump noise. Never put hands near couplings or rotating parts, and if in doubt, stop the machine and inspect fittings, hoses and clamps with the system isolated.
### Will a cavitated pump need to be replaced?
Not always, but cavitation erodes internal surfaces and can lead to rapid wear. A pump that has suffered significant cavitation should be inspected. Even if it is still working, its efficiency may be reduced. Whatever the outcome, correct the inlet conditions first — otherwise a new or repaired pump will suffer the same damage.
### Does aeration affect hydraulic oil?
Yes. Entrained air accelerates oxidation of the oil, causes overheating and can create localised high temperatures that darken and degrade the oil. Aerated oil also makes actuators spongy and less precise. Fixing air leaks, maintaining oil level and improving reservoir design protect both the oil and the components.

## signs-hydraulic-pump-failing
### Can a hydraulic pump fail suddenly without warning?
Sudden failures do happen — for example, from severe cavitation, running dry or a mechanical break — but most pumps show warning signs first, such as noise, heat, slower cycles or increased case drain flow. Regular observation and simple measurements catch many problems early enough to plan a repair.
### What is a healthy case drain flow?
Manufacturers often publish expected leakage values for new pumps, but the most practical approach is to measure case drain flow when the pump is known to be healthy and use that as a baseline. Repeat measurements under the same pressure, speed and temperature conditions. A steady increase indicates wear.
### Could low pressure be caused by something other than the pump?
Yes. A relief valve set too low or stuck open, internal leakage in valves or cylinders, or a faulty pressure compensator can all cause low pressure. Remember that pumps create flow; pressure only builds when flow meets resistance. Test systematically before replacing the pump.
### How does oil analysis help detect pump wear?
Oil analysis can show increasing levels of wear metals such as iron, copper or other elements used in pump components. Rising particle counts and changes in oil condition can also point to wear or contamination. Analysed regularly, it can give early warning before noise or performance changes appear.
### Should I replace the pump or repair it?
Compare the cost and lead time of repair against a new pump, consider the pump's age and availability, and think about efficiency upgrades. For larger and more expensive pumps, professional repair with performance testing is often good value. For small, inexpensive gear pumps, replacement is often simpler.

## hydraulic-motors-explained
### What is a low-speed high-torque (LSHT) motor?
An LSHT motor is designed to deliver very high torque at low rotational speeds, often eliminating the need for a gearbox. Radial piston and orbital motors are common types. They are used for winches, slewing drives, augers, wheel drives and conveyors, where large torque is needed at modest speeds.
### Can a hydraulic motor hold a suspended load?
Not on its own. Hydraulic motors have internal leakage, so a load can slowly rotate the motor and lower over time. Winches and hoists use spring-applied, hydraulically released brakes and counterbalance or brake valves to hold and control loads safely.
### Why does my hydraulic motor run slower under load?
As load increases, pressure rises and internal leakage in the motor and pump increases, so less oil does useful work and speed falls. Some speed drop is normal; a growing drop indicates wear. Check case drain flow, oil temperature and pump performance to identify the cause.
### Do all hydraulic motors need a case drain?
No. Some motors drain internally to the return port, while others require an external case drain, especially when return line pressure is high or motors are used in series. Always follow the manufacturer's data sheet; incorrect draining can damage shaft seals.
### How do I size a hydraulic motor?
Determine the torque required (including starting torque), the speed range, the available pressure and flow, and the type of load. Then select a displacement that delivers the torque at the available pressure and the speed at the available flow, allowing for efficiency losses. Check shaft load limits if pulleys or sprockets apply side loads.

## hydraulic-pump-repair-reconditioning
### How do I know if a repaired pump has been tested properly?
Ask for a test report. A proper test measures flow at various pressures, verifies control settings such as compensator pressure, checks case drain leakage and confirms there are no leaks or abnormal noises. A repair shop that cannot provide test data may not have tested the pump under realistic conditions.
### Why did my rebuilt pump fail soon after installation?
Common causes include contamination left in the system from the original failure, starting the pump without filling the case, air in the suction line, closed suction valves, incorrect rotation or unresolved cavitation problems. Flushing the system and following the correct start-up procedure prevent most early failures.
### Can any pump be repaired?
Most industrial and mobile pumps can be repaired if parts are available and the housing is not damaged. Some low-cost pumps are designed to be replaced rather than repaired. Obsolete pumps can sometimes be repaired using remanufactured or custom-made parts, but the cost should be compared with a modern replacement.
### What is an exchange pump?
An exchange pump is a reconditioned unit available immediately in exchange for your failed pump. You fit the exchange unit to restore production quickly, and the failed unit is repaired and returned to stock. Exchange programmes are useful for critical machines with standard pump models.
### How long does a pump repair take?
It depends on the pump, the damage found and parts availability. Simple repairs can be quick, while repairs requiring special parts or remanufactured components take longer. Ask for an estimate after inspection, and discuss urgent options if downtime is costly.

## hydraulic-pump-flow-power-calculations
### Why is actual pump flow lower than the calculated flow?
The calculation gives theoretical flow based on displacement and speed. Real pumps have internal leakage that increases with pressure, temperature and wear, so actual flow is lower. Volumetric efficiency describes the ratio of actual to theoretical flow. A drop in actual flow over time is a sign of wear.
### How do I convert bar to psi?
One bar is approximately 14.5 psi. For example, 200 bar is roughly 2,900 psi. Keep units consistent when using formulas, as mixing metric and imperial units is a common source of calculation errors.
### Why does motor power need to be higher than hydraulic power?
Pumps are not perfectly efficient. Mechanical friction and internal leakage mean some input power is lost as heat. Dividing hydraulic power by overall efficiency gives the shaft power needed from the motor. Additional margin may be needed for starting loads and duty cycle.
### How do I calculate cylinder retraction speed?
Use the same speed formula but with the annulus area — the piston area minus the rod area. Because the annulus is smaller, the same flow produces a faster retraction speed. Remember that the oil leaving the cap side during retraction can exceed pump flow, which affects return line and valve sizing.
### Can these formulas be used for safety-critical design?
They are useful for estimates and checks, but final designs for safety-critical systems should be prepared by qualified engineers using manufacturer data, appropriate safety factors and applicable standards. Real systems include pressure losses, dynamic loads and efficiency variations that simple formulas do not capture.

## variable-displacement-pumps-energy-efficiency
### What is a pressure-compensated pump?
A pressure-compensated pump delivers full flow until system pressure reaches a set value, then reduces displacement to maintain that pressure with only the flow required. When actuators stop, the pump delivers little flow while holding pressure, saving energy compared with a fixed pump dumping flow over a relief valve.
### Is load sensing worth it on industrial machines?
Load sensing is very common in mobile hydraulics and can benefit industrial machines with multiple functions operating at different pressures. It adjusts pump pressure to just above the highest load demand. The benefit depends on the circuit and duty cycle; an engineering review can estimate savings.
### Can a variable speed drive replace a variable pump?
In some applications, yes. Variable speed drives adjust the electric motor speed to match demand, reducing energy use even with a fixed pump. Combining a variable speed drive with a variable pump is also possible. The best choice depends on the duty cycle, response requirements and budget.
### Why is my variable pump unstable or noisy?
Instability can result from incorrect compensator or load-sensing settings, poor coordination with relief valves, air in the system, or wear in the pump control. Check settings against specifications and ensure the system is properly bled. Persistent problems may need a pump inspection.

## hydraulic-pump-start-up-procedure
### Why must the pump case be filled before start-up?
Piston pumps rely on oil in the case to lubricate bearings and sliding surfaces. Starting with a dry case can cause immediate damage. Filling the case through the uppermost drain port before start-up ensures lubrication from the first revolution.
### How do I check the pump's rotation direction?
Check the arrow on the pump body or the manufacturer's data, then briefly jog the motor and observe the shaft or coupling direction. Running a pump in the wrong direction can damage it and will not produce flow. Electric motors can be reversed by changing phase connections, done by a qualified electrician.
### Why should a new pump be started at low pressure?
Starting at low pressure allows air to be purged, lets you check for leaks and noise, and ensures components are lubricated before full load is applied. Gradually increasing pressure to the set values reduces the risk of damage during commissioning.
### What should I record after commissioning a pump?
Record pressure settings, operating temperature, cycle times and — for piston pumps — case drain flow. These baseline readings make future troubleshooting easier and help identify wear trends early.

## hydraulic-motor-case-drain
### What happens if the case drain is connected to the return line?
If the return line has significant back pressure, the motor's case pressure rises. This can blow shaft seals and damage internal components. Case drains should generally run directly to the tank with minimal restriction, unless the manufacturer specifically allows a different arrangement.
### Can I put a filter on the case drain line?
Sometimes, but only if it is sized generously and monitored, because a blocked filter would raise case pressure. Many systems avoid case drain filters for this reason. Follow the component manufacturer's guidance.
### How often should case drain flow be measured?
For critical piston pumps and motors, periodic measurement — for example during scheduled maintenance — provides valuable trend data. Measure under consistent conditions of pressure, speed and temperature so results can be compared accurately.
### Why is my motor's shaft seal leaking?
Common causes include excessive case pressure from restricted drains, worn seals, shaft damage, or misalignment and side loads on the shaft. Check the case drain first, as replacing the seal without solving high case pressure will lead to repeat failures.
### Does a hot motor case always mean the motor is worn?
Not always. A hot case may result from restricted drain flow, poor case flushing, high system temperatures or heavy duty. However, a case that runs significantly hotter than before, combined with increasing case drain flow, strongly suggests internal wear.

## essential-crane-spare-parts
### How many spare wheels should I keep for a crane?
It depends on the crane's criticality, duty and lead time for replacements. For critical cranes with long lead times, many maintenance teams hold at least a matched set of driven wheels, or complete wheel assemblies, so a worn set can be replaced during a short shutdown. For less critical cranes, a supplier who can manufacture quickly may be enough.
### Should I keep wire rope in stock?
For cranes where rope replacement is planned or rope damage would stop production, keeping a correctly specified rope — or having a supplier who can deliver quickly — is sensible. Store rope correctly: off the floor, in a dry area and protected from damage. Always confirm rope specification against the crane manufacturer's requirements.
### How do I handle obsolete crane parts?
Identify obsolete parts before they fail. Mechanical components such as wheels, pins, sheaves and couplings can often be reverse-engineered and manufactured to approved drawings. Electrical and control components may need modern equivalents or upgrades. Planning ahead avoids emergency shutdowns.
### How do I justify spare parts costs to management?
Compare the cost of holding the spare with the cost of downtime if the part fails and must be sourced. For critical equipment, a single avoided shutdown can pay for spares many times over. Use breakdown history and lead times to support your case.
### How should brake linings be stored?
Keep them in a cool, dry place away from oil, grease, direct sunlight and heat. Store them in original packaging, labelled with the crane and brake type. Contaminated linings lose friction and should not be fitted.

## eot-crane-maintenance-checklist
### Who is allowed to carry out the thorough examination of a crane?
Thorough examinations are typically carried out by a competent person with appropriate training, knowledge and experience, as defined by local regulations. Requirements vary between countries, so check the regulations that apply to your site and use qualified inspectors.
### How often do overhead cranes need a thorough examination?
Intervals are set by local regulations and the crane's use, and may be more frequent for heavy-duty cranes or cranes lifting people. Many regions require periodic examinations at defined intervals and after significant repairs or modifications. Your crane inspector or local regulations will specify the exact requirements.
### What should operators do if they find a defect?
Report it immediately using your site's reporting procedure and, if the defect affects safety, take the crane out of service and prevent its use until it is repaired. Operators should never attempt repairs they are not authorised or trained to carry out.
### Do I need to load test a crane after repairs?
Load testing may be required after major repairs, modifications or replacement of load-bearing components, depending on regulations and the nature of the work. A competent person should decide whether a load test is needed.
### What records should be kept for each crane?
Keep inspection reports, thorough examination certificates, maintenance and repair records, parts replaced, load test certificates and any modifications. Good records demonstrate compliance and help identify recurring problems.

## crane-sheave-inspection-wear
### What is a sheave gauge?
A sheave gauge is a template shaped to the correct groove profile for a specific rope diameter. Placed in the groove, it shows whether the groove has worn too narrow, too wide or into an incorrect shape. Gauges are a simple, effective tool for routine sheave inspection.
### Can I re-machine a worn sheave groove?
Sometimes. If enough material remains and the sheave design allows, grooves can be re-machined to the correct profile. However, re-machining reduces material thickness and changes pitch diameter slightly, so limits must be respected. Cracked or heavily worn sheaves should be replaced.
### Why does a new wire rope wear quickly?
A common reason is fitting a new rope onto worn or corrugated sheaves and drums. The damaged groove profile crushes or abrades the new rope. Inspect and, if necessary, replace sheaves and check the drum whenever a rope is replaced.
### What does a noisy sheave indicate?
Noise often indicates worn or poorly lubricated bearings. A sheave that does not rotate freely forces the rope to slide across the groove, causing rapid wear of both rope and sheave. Investigate and repair promptly.
### Can I use a different sheave from another crane?
Only if it matches the required rope diameter, groove profile, pitch diameter, bore, bearing arrangement and material specification for your crane. Using an incorrect sheave can damage the rope and compromise safety. Check with the crane manufacturer or a qualified engineer.

## crane-brake-maintenance
### How do I know when brake linings need replacing?
Measure lining thickness and compare it with the manufacturer's minimum. Signs of worn linings include longer stopping distances, load drift, noise and the need for frequent adjustment. Replace linings in sets so braking remains even.
### What is the air gap on an electromagnetic brake?
The air gap is the clearance between the armature and the magnet body when the brake is applied. As linings wear, the gap increases. If it becomes too large, the magnet may not release the brake fully or may release slowly. Adjust the gap according to the manufacturer's specification during maintenance.
### Why is oil on a brake dangerous?
Oil or grease on brake surfaces drastically reduces friction, allowing loads to slip or crane travel to overrun. Find and fix the source, such as a leaking gearbox seal, and replace contaminated linings — cleaning is rarely sufficient.
### Can brakes overheat?
Yes. Frequent inching, dragging brakes, incorrect adjustment and heavy duty can cause overheating, which glazes linings and reduces braking performance. Investigate the cause and adjust operating practices or brake sizing if necessary.
### What should be tested after brake maintenance?
Test that the brake holds the rated load without slipping, that it applies when power is removed and releases fully when energised, and that stopping performance is normal. Follow your site's procedures and any regulatory requirements before returning the crane to service.

## crane-duty-classification
### Where can I find my crane's duty classification?
It is often stated in the crane's documentation, nameplate or design data from the manufacturer. If it is not available, a crane engineer can assess the crane and its usage. Knowing the duty class helps when specifying replacement parts.
### Can I change my crane's duty classification?
Using a crane more intensively than its design classification can lead to accelerated wear and fatigue. Upgrading components may increase capability, but changes to duty, capacity or structure should be assessed by qualified crane engineers and may require re-certification.
### Why do crane wheels wear faster on heavy-duty cranes?
Heavy-duty cranes complete more load cycles with heavier loads, increasing contact stress and rolling wear. Wheels for these cranes need appropriate materials, heat treatment and sometimes larger diameters to achieve acceptable life.
### Do duty classes differ between standards?
Yes. Different standards systems use different designations and categories, but they consider similar factors such as load spectrum and usage. When comparing cranes or components, make sure you compare classifications from the same system or convert carefully.
### How does duty affect replacement part specification?
Higher duty requires stronger materials, better heat treatment, larger safety margins and more robust designs. Informing your supplier of the crane's duty and usage allows them to specify parts that last.

## reverse-engineering-obsolete-crane-parts
### How accurate is reverse engineering from a worn part?
Accuracy depends on the condition of the part and the expertise of the engineers. Worn surfaces must be reconstructed using unworn areas, mating parts and engineering judgement. Providing mating parts or dimensions and an unworn sample, if available, improves accuracy.
### Will I own the drawing created by reverse engineering?
This depends on your agreement with the supplier. Many customers request the approved drawing as part of the order so they can reorder in future. Discuss ownership and confidentiality before work begins.
### Can reverse-engineered parts be better than the original?
Yes. Reverse engineering offers the opportunity to improve materials, heat treatment and design details based on how the original failed. Any changes to load-bearing parts should be reviewed by qualified engineers.
### How long does reverse engineering take?
Simple parts can be measured and drawn quickly, while complex assemblies with many interfaces take longer. Customer approval time also affects the schedule. Planning reverse engineering before failure avoids urgent pressure.
### What parts are not suitable for reverse engineering?
Certified lifting components such as hooks, and some safety-critical or proprietary electronic components, are usually better replaced with certified or OEM equivalents. Discuss suitability with your supplier.

## crane-hook-load-block-inspection
### Why must damaged hooks never be welded?
Hooks are forged and heat-treated to achieve specific strength and toughness. Welding or heating changes the material properties and can create hidden weaknesses or cracks. Damaged hooks must be replaced with correctly rated components.
### How is throat opening measured?
Many inspection programmes record a reference measurement between two points on the hook, sometimes marked with punch marks, when the hook is new. Later measurements are compared with this reference. An increase beyond the allowed limit indicates overload or damage, and the hook must be removed from service.
### What is the purpose of the hook latch?
The latch closes the throat opening to help prevent slings and rigging from slipping off the hook. It is not designed to carry load. A missing or damaged latch should be replaced before the hook is used.
### Do load blocks need NDT inspection?
Non-destructive testing may be required during periodic thorough examinations, particularly for high-duty cranes or after incidents. The competent person conducting the examination determines whether NDT is needed.
### What causes hook swivel bearings to fail?
Lack of lubrication, contamination and overload are common causes. A rough or seized swivel prevents the hook from rotating freely, which can twist ropes and slings. Inspect and lubricate swivels as part of routine maintenance.

## reducing-crane-downtime
### What is the biggest cause of crane downtime?
It varies by site, but common causes include brake problems, electrical faults, wheel and rail wear, rope damage and waiting for spare parts. Analysing your own breakdown records is the best way to identify the main causes at your site.
### How can condition monitoring reduce downtime?
By measuring wear and performance regularly — wheel wear, brake lining thickness, gearbox oil condition, temperatures — you can detect problems before they cause failures and schedule repairs during planned maintenance.
### Is preventive maintenance always better than reactive maintenance?
For critical equipment, planned maintenance is usually far more cost-effective because it avoids unplanned shutdowns. For non-critical equipment with readily available parts, running to failure may sometimes be acceptable. The key is matching the strategy to criticality.
### How does operator training reduce downtime?
Trained operators avoid harsh operation, side pulls and overloading, report defects early and perform effective pre-use checks. These behaviours reduce wear and catch problems before they become breakdowns.
### How often should a spares list be reviewed?
At least annually and after every significant breakdown. Reviewing ensures spares match current equipment, adds parts that caused downtime and removes obsolete items.

## gantry-vs-overhead-crane-maintenance
### Do gantry cranes need more maintenance than overhead cranes?
Outdoor gantry cranes often need more attention to corrosion, weather protection, wind safety systems and power supply cables. The core hoist and trolley maintenance is similar. The overall effort depends on duty, environment and crane design.
### What is a storm brake or rail clamp?
These devices secure outdoor cranes against being moved by strong winds. They must be inspected and tested regularly, and operators must know when and how to secure the crane when wind speeds increase.
### Why do gantry crane rails need regular inspection?
Ground-level rails can be affected by foundation settlement, traffic crossing, debris and corrosion. Misaligned or damaged rails cause wheel wear and skewing. Regular inspection and cleaning protect wheels and travel drives.
### Are different wheels needed for outdoor cranes?
Outdoor cranes may benefit from wheels with improved sealing for bearings, corrosion protection and materials suited to the duty. Specify environmental conditions when ordering replacement wheels.
### What is a semi-gantry crane?
A semi-gantry crane has one leg running on a ground rail and the other end supported on an elevated runway, often on a building. It combines characteristics of gantry and overhead cranes, and maintenance must address both travel systems.
