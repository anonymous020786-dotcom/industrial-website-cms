From excavators and loaders to tower cranes and concrete pumps, construction equipment relies on hydraulics for power and control. Hydraulic systems deliver large forces in compact packages, which makes them ideal for mobile machines. But construction sites are among the harshest environments for hydraulic components.

## Where hydraulics are used on construction sites

- **Excavators:** boom, arm and bucket cylinders; swing and travel motors.
- **Wheel loaders and backhoes:** lift, tilt and steering cylinders.
- **Mobile and crawler cranes:** boom luffing, telescoping, outriggers, winches and slewing.
- **Tower cranes:** climbing systems and some slewing and hoist drives.
- **Concrete pumps:** pumping cylinders and boom cylinders.
- **Piling rigs and drilling equipment:** rotary drives, pull-down and mast cylinders.
- **Dump trucks and tippers:** telescopic tipping cylinders.
- **Aerial work platforms:** lifting and steering functions.

## The challenges

### Contamination

Dust, mud, cement and grit attack rod seals and enter systems during maintenance. Contamination is the leading cause of hydraulic failure on construction equipment.

### Shock loads

Digging, breaking and lifting create sudden pressure spikes and impacts that stress cylinders, pins and structures.

### Weather and temperature

Equipment works in heat, cold, rain and dust. Cold starts thicken oil; hot days push systems towards overheating.

### Rod damage

Exposed cylinder rods are struck by rocks, debris and tools, damaging chrome and seals.

### Operator practices

Running cylinders hard against end stops, overloading and rough operation accelerate wear.

### Time pressure

Projects run on tight schedules, so breakdowns are costly and maintenance windows short.

## Keeping hydraulics reliable on site

| Area | Good practice |
|---|---|
| Daily checks | Look for leaks, damaged hoses, rod damage and oil level |
| Cleanliness | Clean around fittings before opening; cap hoses; filter new oil |
| Rod protection | Use guards on vulnerable cylinders; retract rods when parked |
| Filters | Change on schedule and inspect indicators |
| Pins and bushes | Grease regularly; replace worn pins before they cause misalignment |
| Temperature | Keep coolers clean; warm up machines in cold weather |
| Operator training | Avoid shock loading and bottoming out cylinders |

## Cylinder repair on construction equipment

Construction machines often have custom cylinders with long lead times from original manufacturers. Local cylinder repair and manufacturing can return machines to work faster: rods re-chromed or replaced, barrels honed, seals upgraded and cylinders pressure-tested. When cylinders fail repeatedly, upgrades such as larger rods, improved seals or better rod coatings can help.

## Planning for uptime

- Keep spare seal kits for critical cylinders.
- Maintain a stock of high-wear hoses.
- Identify cylinders with long lead times and consider spares.
- Use oil analysis on high-value machines to catch wear early.

## Lifting equipment on site

Cranes and lifting equipment are subject to strict inspection and certification requirements in most regions. Hydraulic components on lifting equipment — luffing and telescoping cylinders, load-holding valves and winches — are safety-critical and must be maintained and repaired by competent people to the manufacturer's specifications.

Mining equipment works harder than almost any other machinery. Haul trucks, excavators, loaders, drills and crushers operate around the clock, in abrasive dust and mud, with heavy shock loads and limited time for maintenance. Hydraulic components must be designed, specified and maintained for these conditions.

## Where hydraulics are used in mining

- **Excavators and shovels:** boom, stick and bucket cylinders, swing drives.
- **Haul trucks:** steering, hoist (tipping) and suspension systems.
- **Wheel loaders and dozers:** lift, tilt, blade and ripper cylinders.
- **Drill rigs:** feed, rotation and positioning functions.
- **Crushers and screens:** adjustment and clearing cylinders, tramp release systems.
- **Underground equipment:** roof supports, loaders and bolting rigs.

## The challenges

### Abrasive contamination

Fine rock dust is highly abrasive. It wears rod seals and, once inside the system, damages pumps, valves and cylinders.

### Shock and overload

Digging into rock and handling heavy loads create pressure spikes and impact loads far above normal working conditions.

### Continuous operation

High utilisation means wear accumulates quickly and downtime is expensive.

### Corrosion

Water, acidic mine water and chemicals attack rods and structures.

### Heat

High ambient temperatures and continuous operation challenge cooling systems.

## Design features for mining duty

| Feature | Benefit |
|---|---|
| Heavy-wall barrels | Resist bulging and damage from impacts |
| Larger rod diameters | Resist buckling and bending |
| Upgraded rod coatings | Better corrosion and wear resistance |
| Heavy-duty wipers and seal systems | Keep abrasive dust out |
| Robust mountings and spherical bearings | Accommodate misalignment and shock |
| Cushioning | Reduce impact at end of stroke |
| Rod guards or bellows | Protect exposed rods |

## Maintenance practices

- **Inspect rods daily** for damage; damaged rods destroy seals quickly.
- **Keep breathers and filters in good condition** and change elements on condition.
- **Use oil analysis** to detect contamination and wear early.
- **Clean equipment** before maintenance and keep hydraulic openings sealed.
- **Grease pins and bushes** frequently; worn pins cause misalignment and side loading.
- **Track component life** and replace cylinders proactively on critical machines.

## Exchange cylinder programmes

Many mines use exchange programmes: a reconditioned spare cylinder is fitted immediately when one fails or reaches its planned life, and the removed unit is sent for overhaul. This minimises downtime and allows thorough repairs without time pressure.

## Repair findings drive improvement

Each overhauled cylinder tells a story: scored barrels point to contamination, uneven bushing wear to side loads, bent rods to overload or buckling. Tracking these findings across a fleet helps identify improvements in design, maintenance or operation.

## Safety in mining hydraulics

Mining equipment carries large stored energies in hydraulic systems and suspended loads. Isolation procedures, support of raised components and safe release of pressure — including accumulators — are critical whenever maintenance is performed.

Container terminals and bulk ports operate continuously. Ship-to-shore cranes, rubber-tyred and rail-mounted gantry cranes, reach stackers and straddle carriers move containers day and night. When a crane stops, vessel schedules slip, trucks queue and costs mount. Port equipment maintenance is about maximising availability in one of the harshest environments for machinery.

## Equipment in a typical port

- **Ship-to-shore (STS) cranes:** load and unload vessels.
- **Rail-mounted gantry (RMG) cranes:** stack containers in yards.
- **Rubber-tyred gantry (RTG) cranes:** flexible yard stacking.
- **Mobile harbour cranes:** multi-purpose cargo handling.
- **Reach stackers and straddle carriers:** container movement.
- **Bulk handling equipment:** grab cranes, ship loaders and conveyors.

## The challenges

### Continuous duty

Port cranes often fall into high duty classes. Wheels, ropes, brakes and drives accumulate wear quickly.

### Salt and corrosion

Salt-laden air corrodes structures, fasteners, electrical connections and exposed machined surfaces.

### Wind

High winds can affect crane stability. Storm protection systems, rail clamps and anchors are critical.

### Scale and height

Inspecting large structures at height requires planning, access equipment and safe working procedures.

### Time pressure

Maintenance windows must fit around vessel schedules.

## Key maintenance areas

| Area | Focus |
|---|---|
| Wheels and rails | Wear monitoring, alignment, matched wheel replacement |
| Wire ropes and sheaves | Regular inspection, lubrication, planned replacement |
| Brakes | Hoist, trolley, gantry and storm brakes — testing and adjustment |
| Spreaders | Twistlocks, hydraulics and electrical systems |
| Hydraulics | Spreader, boom hoist and brake systems |
| Structures | Corrosion and fatigue crack inspection |
| Electrical | Cable reels, slip rings, drives and enclosures |

## Wheels: a frequent consumable

Gantry and trolley wheels on port cranes work hard. Matching driven wheels, maintaining rail alignment and choosing hardened wheels suited to the duty significantly extend wheel life. Holding spare wheel assemblies allows quick exchanges during short maintenance windows.

## Corrosion management

- Maintain paint systems on structures and repair damage promptly.
- Use corrosion-resistant fasteners where appropriate.
- Seal electrical enclosures and inspect regularly.
- Protect hydraulic cylinder rods with suitable coatings.
- Wash down equipment to remove salt deposits where practical.

## Planning for availability

1. **Criticality ranking** of each crane and component.
2. **Condition monitoring:** rope inspection, wheel wear, oil analysis, thermography.
3. **Critical spares** on site: wheels, brake components, spreader parts, hydraulic components.
4. **Planned maintenance windows** coordinated with operations.
5. **Root cause analysis** for repeat failures.

## Spreaders and hydraulics

Container spreaders rely on hydraulic and electrical systems for twistlocks, telescoping and flippers. They take constant impacts, so hydraulic cylinders, hoses and valves on spreaders need frequent inspection and fast replacement.
