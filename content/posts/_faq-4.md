# FAQ add-ons — industry applications, buying guides

## hydraulics-construction-equipment
### Why do hydraulic cylinders fail more often on construction sites?
Construction sites combine almost every condition that damages hydraulics: abrasive dust and cement, mud and water, impacts from rocks and debris, shock loads from digging and breaking, and maintenance that often happens outdoors under time pressure. Exposed cylinder rods are particularly vulnerable. Protecting rods, keeping wipers and filters in good condition, and cleaning fittings before opening the system make a noticeable difference to cylinder life.
### How can I protect cylinder rods on site?
Retract rods whenever machines are parked, fit rod guards or bellows on cylinders exposed to falling debris, clean off cement and mud regularly, and protect rods when welding nearby. Inspect rods during daily walk-arounds and arrange repairs quickly when damage appears, because a nicked rod will quickly destroy the rod seal and let contamination into the system.
### How often should hydraulic oil be changed on construction equipment?
Follow the machine manufacturer's recommended interval, and shorten it if equipment works in extremely dusty, hot or wet conditions. Oil analysis is the best way to decide: it shows contamination, water content and oil condition, so you change oil when it is actually needed rather than too early or too late. Always filter new oil when filling.
### Can a local workshop repair cylinders from branded construction machines?
In most cases, yes. Experienced cylinder shops can dismantle, inspect, repair and test cylinders from all major equipment brands, often faster than waiting for original replacement units. They can also manufacture replacement parts such as rods, glands and pistons. For machines under warranty, check the manufacturer's warranty conditions first.
### What should be included in a daily hydraulic check?
Look for leaks around cylinders, hoses and fittings; check hoses for abrasion, kinks and bulges; inspect rods for damage; check the oil level and filter indicators; and note any unusual noises, slow movement or drift. Report problems immediately — catching a damaged hose or rod early prevents bigger failures and safety risks.

## hydraulic-components-mining
### What makes mining hydraulics different from other industries?
Mining combines continuous high-utilisation operation, heavy shock loads, abrasive dust and corrosive water. Components accumulate wear far faster than in most industrial settings, and downtime is very expensive. As a result, mining cylinders often use heavier walls, larger rods, upgraded seals and coatings, and mines frequently plan replacements based on component life rather than waiting for failure.
### What is a component exchange programme?
An exchange programme keeps reconditioned spare components — cylinders, pumps or motors — ready to fit. When a unit fails or reaches its planned life, the spare is installed immediately and the removed unit is sent for overhaul. This keeps machines working and allows thorough repairs without production pressure.
### How can repair reports improve reliability?
Each overhaul report records what failed and why: scored barrels, bent rods, uneven bushing wear, contamination or seal damage. Tracking these findings across a fleet reveals patterns, such as recurring contamination or side loading. Addressing these root causes through better filtration, design upgrades or operating practices reduces future failures.
### Are rod bellows effective in mining?
Rod bellows and guards can protect exposed rods from impacts and abrasive material in suitable applications. They must be durable, correctly sized and maintained, because a torn bellow can trap debris against the rod. In some applications, robust rod coatings and heavy-duty wipers are more practical.
### What safety precautions apply to mining hydraulics?
Hydraulic systems on mining equipment store large energies in pressurised oil, accumulators and raised components such as buckets and truck bodies. Maintenance must follow strict isolation procedures: lower or mechanically support raised parts, relieve pressure, discharge accumulators and verify zero energy before work begins.

## port-crane-maintenance
### Why do port cranes need special maintenance planning?
Port cranes operate continuously, often in high duty classes, in salty, corrosive air and exposed to wind. Maintenance windows must fit around vessel schedules. This makes condition monitoring, critical spares and carefully planned maintenance windows essential to keep cranes available.
### How can corrosion on port cranes be controlled?
Maintain protective coatings and repair damage promptly, use corrosion-resistant fasteners and enclosures where appropriate, wash salt deposits from accessible areas, protect hydraulic cylinder rods with suitable coatings and inspect structures regularly for corrosion and fatigue cracks. Corrosion control is ongoing work rather than a one-time task.
### Which spares are most important for port cranes?
Typical critical spares include wheel assemblies, brake components, wire rope or reliable rope supply, spreader parts such as twistlocks and hydraulic components, drive components and key electrical items. The exact list depends on crane type, duty and supplier lead times.
### How are port cranes protected from wind?
Storm brakes, rail clamps, anchors and tie-downs secure cranes against high winds, while anemometers and alarms warn operators. These systems must be inspected and tested regularly, and operators must follow procedures for securing cranes when wind limits are approached.
### Why do spreaders need frequent attention?
Spreaders take repeated impacts as they lock onto containers, and their hydraulic and electrical systems operate constantly. Twistlocks, cylinders, hoses and sensors should be inspected frequently, and spare spreader components held so faults can be fixed quickly.

## warehouse-crane-hoist-reliability
### Why do hoist brakes wear quickly in warehouses?
High-cycle operations involve frequent starts, stops and inching movements. Each stop uses the brake and generates heat. Inching in particular causes rapid wear. Operator training, smooth controls such as variable frequency drives and regular brake adjustment extend brake life.
### What is the difference between a chain hoist and a wire rope hoist?
Chain hoists are compact and economical for lighter loads and shorter lifts. Wire rope hoists suit heavier loads, longer lifts and higher speeds, and are common on overhead cranes. The choice depends on capacity, lift height, duty and space.
### How can I tell if a crane is overworked for its duty?
Signs include overheating motors, rapid wear of brakes, wheels and ropes, frequent electrical faults and structural cracking. If operations have increased since the crane was installed, review whether the crane's duty classification still matches its use.
### Are radio remote controls safer than pendants?
Radio remotes allow operators to stand away from the load and choose the best viewing position, which can improve safety. They must be properly maintained, tested and used according to procedures, with clear rules for who controls the crane.
### What maintenance records should be kept for warehouse cranes?
Keep pre-use check records, inspection and thorough examination reports, repair and parts records, and any load test certificates. These records demonstrate compliance and help identify recurring issues.

## hydraulic-press-maintenance
### Why does a hydraulic press ram drift down?
Drift is usually caused by internal leakage in the main cylinder or valves, or problems with counterbalance or safety valves. Because presses can be dangerous, the cause should be diagnosed promptly by trained personnel and the ram mechanically supported during work.
### What is a prefill valve?
A prefill valve allows large press cylinders to fill quickly from the reservoir during the fast approach stroke, using minimal pump flow. During the pressing stroke it closes so the pump can build pressure. Faulty prefill valves can cause slow approach, poor pressing or shock.
### How important is oil cleanliness for presses?
Very important, particularly for presses with proportional or servo valves. Contamination causes valve sticking, poor control and seal wear. Fine filtration, clean oil handling and oil analysis are key parts of press maintenance.
### Should press safety systems be tested regularly?
Yes. Guards, interlocks, light curtains, two-hand controls, safety valves and emergency stops must be inspected and tested according to the manufacturer's instructions and applicable regulations. Never bypass safety devices.
### Can old presses be upgraded?
Yes. Older presses can be upgraded with modern power units, proportional control, energy-efficient pumps and improved safety systems. Upgrades should be engineered to match the press structure and intended use.

## hydraulics-steel-plants-foundries
### Why are fire-resistant hydraulic fluids used in steel plants?
A hydraulic leak spraying onto molten metal, hot slabs or furnace surfaces can ignite. Fire-resistant fluids are much less likely to sustain a fire, reducing that risk in high-hazard areas. Switching fluid type is a significant change: seals, hoses, filters, paints and sometimes pumps must be compatible, the system usually needs thorough flushing, and maintenance practices such as monitoring water content may change. A risk assessment should decide where fire-resistant fluids are needed.
### How can hydraulic hoses be protected from radiant heat?
Route hoses away from heat sources wherever possible, add heat shields between hot equipment and hydraulic lines, use heat-resistant protective sleeves and select hoses rated for the temperatures involved. Hoses in hot zones age faster than elsewhere, so inspect them more often and replace them proactively rather than waiting for leaks.
### Where does water contamination come from in mill hydraulics?
Cooling water sprays, steam, humid air drawn in through breathers, leaking heat exchangers and washing all introduce water. Water causes corrosion, degrades oil and damages pumps and valves. Desiccant breathers, good rod wipers and seals, cooler maintenance and regular oil analysis to measure water content help keep it under control.
### Why do roll gap control systems need exceptionally clean oil?
Roll gap and other precision systems use servo or high-response proportional valves with very small internal clearances. Particles cause sticking, drifting and erratic control, which directly affects product thickness and quality. Fine pressure filtration, offline filtration and strict cleanliness targets are standard for these systems.
### How can hydraulic downtime in steel plants be reduced?
Plan cylinder and valve overhauls during scheduled mill shutdowns, hold spares for critical actuators, monitor oil condition and temperatures, and investigate every repeat failure for root causes such as heat, scale ingress or misalignment. Exchange cylinders that can be fitted quickly are particularly valuable on mill equipment.

## mobile-crane-hydraulic-systems
### What does a counterbalance valve do on a mobile crane?
A counterbalance valve sits between the control valve and the cylinder or winch motor. It holds the load in position when the control is released and controls the speed of lowering so the load cannot run away. If a hose bursts, it helps prevent the load from dropping. Because it is safety-critical, it must only be adjusted, repaired or replaced according to the crane manufacturer's instructions.
### Why do outrigger jacks slowly creep?
Creep is usually caused by internal leakage across the jack cylinder's piston seal or by leakage in the pilot-operated check valve that holds the jack. Since outriggers carry a large share of the crane's weight and load, any creep should be investigated before the crane is used again.
### Can mobile crane cylinders be repaired by a local workshop?
Yes, if the workshop has experience with crane cylinders and follows the manufacturer's specifications. A proper repair includes inspection and measurement of all parts, correct seal kits, rod and barrel repairs where needed, pressure and function testing with documented results, and checking or replacing the load-holding valves.
### What causes jerky boom telescoping?
Typical causes include air in the hydraulic system, contamination, worn seals or guide bearings in the telescopic cylinder, and dry or worn slide pads between boom sections. Jerky motion affects load control and can indicate developing damage, so it should be diagnosed promptly.
### Are hydraulic components covered in crane inspections?
Yes. Periodic inspections and thorough examinations required by lifting equipment regulations typically include hydraulic cylinders, hoses, fittings, load-holding valves and associated safety systems such as load moment indicators. Keep records of hydraulic repairs alongside the crane's inspection history.

## retrofitting-industrial-equipment-hydraulics
### How do I decide between a retrofit and a new machine?
If the machine's frame, structure and mechanical components are sound and only the hydraulics and controls are outdated or unreliable, a retrofit is usually more economical and faster than replacing the machine. If the structure is worn out, the machine cannot meet new process requirements, or safety upgrades would be impractical, a new machine may be better. A survey of the existing equipment provides the facts to make this decision.
### How long does a hydraulic retrofit take?
Engineering, manufacturing and testing the new power unit and assemblies happen off-site while the machine keeps running. Installation can often be completed during a planned shutdown because hoses, pipework and wiring are prepared in advance. The exact duration depends on the machine size and complexity.
### Can a retrofit really reduce energy costs?
On machines that run long hours, yes. Replacing fixed pumps that dump excess flow over relief valves with variable displacement pumps or variable speed drives, adding accumulators and reducing throttling losses can cut energy consumption and heat generation substantially. Lower oil temperatures also extend seal and oil life.
### What documentation should I receive after a retrofit?
Updated hydraulic circuit diagrams, electrical drawings, parts lists, pressure and flow settings, commissioning records and operating and maintenance instructions. Good documentation makes future troubleshooting faster and reduces dependence on the original installer.
### Can safety be improved as part of a retrofit?
Yes. Retrofits are an ideal opportunity to add load-holding valves, monitored safety valves, pressure monitoring, accumulator safety blocks and modern control functions, alongside guarding and control system upgrades required by current safety expectations.

## tipper-dump-truck-hydraulic-cylinders
### Why do tipper trucks tip over during unloading?
Overturning usually happens when the body is raised on uneven or soft ground, when sticky material stays high in the body instead of sliding out, when loads are unevenly distributed, or in strong crosswinds. Each of these shifts the centre of gravity sideways as the body rises. Tipping only on firm, level ground and confirming the load discharges freely are the most important precautions.
### Is it safe to work under a raised tipper body?
Only when the body is held by a suitable mechanical body prop, correctly positioned and secured. Hydraulics alone must never be trusted to hold a raised body during maintenance, because valves can leak, seals can fail and hoses can burst.
### Why does a raised tipper body slowly lower by itself?
Drift is typically caused by leakage in the tipping control valve or by internal leakage in the cylinder stages. It can worsen over time and may indicate seals or valves near the end of their life, so investigate and repair it promptly.
### How often should tipper cylinders be serviced?
Follow the vehicle or cylinder manufacturer's recommendations and inspect cylinders regularly for leaks, damaged stages and worn mountings. Tippers working in quarries, construction and demolition sites, or carrying sticky and abrasive loads, generally need more frequent attention than those carrying free-flowing materials.
### Can a tipper cylinder be upgraded for heavier work?
Yes. Heavier-duty cylinders with larger stages, improved bearings and upgraded seals are available for demanding applications. The replacement must suit the body design, mounting geometry, load and vehicle, so discuss the application with the cylinder manufacturer before upgrading.

## how-to-write-rfq-hydraulic-components
### What is the single most important part of an RFQ?
Clear technical requirements. Suppliers need dimensions or drawings, pressures, loads, speeds, operating conditions, quantities and documentation needs to quote accurately. Missing technical information is the most common reason quotes are delayed, vary widely between suppliers, or lead to parts that do not fit or perform as expected.
### Should I include photos with my RFQ?
Yes. Photos of the part, its installation, mountings, port orientations and any nameplates help suppliers understand the requirement quickly and reduce questions — especially when drawings are missing. Include a tape measure or ruler in shots for scale where possible.
### How do I compare quotations fairly?
Check that each quote meets the full specification, includes the testing and documentation you requested, states a realistic lead time and warranty, and lists any assumptions or exclusions. Put the quotes side by side on these points before comparing price. A cheaper quote that excludes testing or arrives weeks later may cost more overall.
### Should spare parts be quoted in the same RFQ?
Yes. Asking for seal kits, wear parts or a complete spare unit at the same time often reduces cost, because the supplier can manufacture or order them alongside the main item. It also ensures spares are available when you need them.
### What if I do not know all the technical details?
Send what you have — photos, partial dimensions, the machine type and a description of how the part is used. A capable supplier will help you complete the specification, measure the part or reverse-engineer it. It is better to start the conversation early than wait for perfect information.

## choosing-hydraulic-cylinder-manufacturer
### Why does it matter whether a manufacturer works in-house?
Machining, honing, welding, assembly and testing done in-house give the manufacturer direct control over quality and lead times. Outsourced processes can still work well, but they add handovers and scheduling dependencies, so ask how they are controlled and inspected.
### Should every cylinder be pressure tested before delivery?
Yes. Pressure and function testing confirms that seals are correctly installed, welds and threads hold pressure, and the cylinder strokes and cushions properly. A documented test record should be available for every unit, new or repaired.
### What warranty should I expect from a cylinder supplier?
Terms vary, so ask what is covered, for how long and under what conditions. Warranties usually exclude damage from contamination, overload, misalignment or improper installation. A supplier confident in their work will explain the terms clearly and investigate any failures openly.
### Can a manufacturer improve on my existing cylinder design?
A manufacturer with engineering capability can review how and why the existing cylinder failed and recommend improvements — a larger rod to resist buckling, better seals for heat or speed, harder rod coatings for corrosive environments, or improved mountings to tolerate misalignment.
### Is it worth visiting a supplier before ordering?
For significant or ongoing business, yes. A visit shows you the workshop's equipment, cleanliness, organisation, testing facilities and the people you will work with — all strong indicators of the quality you can expect.

## oem-vs-aftermarket-crane-parts
### Are aftermarket crane parts safe to use?
Quality aftermarket parts made to the correct specification — with appropriate materials, heat treatment and inspection — can perform as well as or better than original parts. Quality varies between suppliers, so choose experienced manufacturers, request documentation, and ensure load-bearing parts meet or exceed the original specification.
### Will aftermarket parts affect my crane's warranty?
They may. For cranes still under warranty or a service agreement, check the terms before fitting non-OEM parts. For older cranes outside warranty, aftermarket parts are widely used for mechanical wear components.
### Which crane parts should always be OEM or certified?
Certified lifting components such as hooks and rated lifting accessories, some safety devices, and proprietary electronic controls are usually best sourced from the OEM or as certified equivalents. Mechanical parts such as wheels, pins, sheaves and couplings are commonly sourced from quality aftermarket manufacturers.
### How can I check the quality of aftermarket parts?
Request dimensional inspection reports, hardness test results and material certificates; ask about the supplier's engineering and manufacturing processes; and, for new suppliers, consider a workshop visit or references from other crane users.
### Why keep drawings for aftermarket parts?
Approved drawings let you reorder quickly, compare suppliers, and avoid repeating reverse engineering every time a part wears out. They also provide a record of specifications for audits and future maintenance.

## total-cost-of-ownership-hydraulic-equipment
### What costs make up total cost of ownership?
Purchase and installation, energy consumption, routine maintenance (filters, oil, seals, labour), repairs and spare parts, production losses during downtime, and end-of-life disposal or refurbishment. For many hydraulic systems, energy and downtime together far exceed the purchase price.
### Why is energy often the biggest running cost?
Hydraulic power units on machines running many hours consume significant electricity, and inefficient circuits waste much of it as heat. That heat then requires coolers and shortens oil and seal life — adding further cost. Efficient pump and control choices reduce both energy and maintenance costs.
### How should I put a value on downtime?
Estimate what an hour or day of stoppage costs for the affected process: lost output or margin, idle labour, overtime to catch up, late-delivery penalties and knock-on effects on other operations. Even a rough figure usually shows that reliability improvements pay back quickly on critical equipment.
### Do efficient hydraulic systems always cost more to buy?
Often they cost more upfront because variable pumps, better valves and controls are more expensive than basic components. On high-utilisation machines, energy savings and improved reliability commonly recover that difference over the equipment's life.
### Can suppliers help me compare total cost of ownership?
Yes. Ask suppliers for expected energy consumption, maintenance intervals, component life and spare parts costs. This information lets you compare options on lifetime value rather than purchase price alone.

## lead-times-custom-components
### Why do custom components take longer than catalogue parts?
Catalogue parts may be in stock, while custom components require engineering, drawing approval, material procurement, machining, heat treatment or coatings, assembly and testing. Each stage takes time, and bought-in items such as pumps or special steels can add their own lead times.
### How can I get an urgent part faster?
Send complete information immediately, ask whether repairing the existing part is faster than making a new one, accept equivalent standard components where suitable, approve drawings quickly and explain the cost of downtime so the supplier can prioritise. Partial deliveries — one urgent unit first — can also help.
### Do approval delays really affect delivery dates?
Yes. Manufacturing usually cannot start until drawings are approved, so every day spent waiting adds directly to the lead time. Nominate someone with authority to review and approve drawings promptly.
### Should I hold spares for components with long lead times?
For critical equipment, holding spares for long-lead components — custom cylinders, special pumps, matched crane wheels — is often the most effective way to prevent long shutdowns. The cost of the spare is usually small compared with weeks of downtime.
### Are very short promised lead times reliable?
Not always. Ask how the supplier will meet the date, which materials and components they already have, and how they will keep you updated. A realistic date that is kept is more valuable than an optimistic one that slips.

## inspection-documentation-supplier
### When should I ask suppliers for documentation?
At the RFQ stage. Some documentation — such as inspection reports, hardness tests or material certificates — requires specific steps during manufacturing and affects price. Requesting it after the order may be impossible or expensive.
### What is a dimensional inspection report?
It is a record of measured dimensions compared with the drawing's requirements and tolerances, usually covering critical dimensions. It confirms the part was checked and is within tolerance, and provides baseline data for future wear measurements.
### Why are material certificates important?
They document the chemical composition and mechanical properties of the material used, providing traceability back to the steel batch. For load-bearing and safety-critical components, they are key evidence that the correct material was used.
### Should repaired components come with documentation?
Yes. A repair findings report explains what was found and why the component failed, and a test record confirms it was tested after repair. This information helps you prevent repeat failures and supports maintenance records.
### How should documentation be stored?
Attach it to the equipment record in your maintenance management system or a well-organised file, linked to the machine and component. When a component is replaced or repaired, previous documentation becomes valuable reference for specifications and wear history.

## spare-parts-inventory-planning
### How do I identify which spare parts are critical?
Ask three questions for each component: does its failure stop production or create a safety risk, is there an alternative way to keep operating, and how long would a replacement take to obtain? Components that stop critical equipment and have long lead times are the strongest candidates for stock.
### What is an insurance spare?
An insurance spare is a part that rarely fails but would cause a long, expensive shutdown if it did — for example, a gearbox, a custom cylinder or a matched set of crane wheels. It may sit on the shelf for years, but one avoided shutdown can justify its cost.
### How do I stop spare parts deteriorating in storage?
Protect machined surfaces from corrosion, store seals and rubber parts away from heat, light and ozone, keep cylinders horizontal with ports capped and rods protected, rotate shafts periodically where recommended, and label everything clearly with the equipment it belongs to.
### How often should the spares list be reviewed?
At least once a year and after every significant breakdown. Add parts that caused unexpected downtime, adjust quantities based on actual usage, and remove items for equipment that has been retired or replaced.
### Can suppliers hold stock on my behalf?
Many manufacturers offer customer-specific stock, framework agreements or exchange programmes for critical components. These arrangements reduce your on-site inventory while guaranteeing availability when parts are needed.

## preparing-sample-reverse-engineering
### Should I clean a part before sending it for reverse engineering?
Remove loose dirt and excess oil so the part can be handled safely, but avoid grinding, wire brushing or polishing. Aggressive cleaning can change critical dimensions and remove wear patterns that help engineers understand how the part failed.
### Do I need to send the mating parts too?
If practical, yes. Mating parts show the fits and clearances the new part must achieve. If they cannot be removed, measure their key dimensions — bore and shaft diameters, thread sizes, mounting positions — and include those measurements with photos.
### What if the part is broken into pieces?
Send all the pieces. The fracture surfaces often reveal how and why the part failed — fatigue, overload or material defects — which can guide improvements to material, heat treatment or design in the replacement.
### Can a part be measured on site instead of sent away?
For parts that cannot be removed, or very large components, on-site measurement is possible. However, complex parts with precise fits are best measured in a workshop with proper equipment, so remove and send them if downtime allows.
### Will my original part be returned?
Usually, if you request it. Agree this when sending the part, and label it clearly with your company name and reference so it can be identified and returned after the drawing has been completed.

## hydraulic-safety-basics
### What is a hydraulic injection injury?
It occurs when high-pressure fluid from a pinhole leak or failed fitting penetrates the skin. The wound may look small, but fluid can travel deep into tissue and cause severe damage. Treat any suspected injection injury as a medical emergency, seek specialist treatment immediately and tell medical staff the injury involved hydraulic fluid.
### Is a hydraulic system safe once the pump is switched off?
Not necessarily. Pressure can remain trapped behind check valves and in cylinders, accumulators store pressurised oil by design, and raised loads held by cylinders can fall if pressure is released. Always relieve pressure, discharge accumulators, support loads mechanically and verify zero energy before starting work.
### How should I look for hydraulic leaks safely?
Never use your hands. Pass a piece of cardboard or wood near suspected leaks while wearing eye protection and gloves, and keep your body out of line with fittings and hoses. If a leak is found, shut down and isolate the system before repairing it.
### Why must loads be supported mechanically before maintenance?
Hydraulic cylinders and valves can leak internally, and hoses can fail without warning. A load held only by hydraulics can drift down or drop suddenly. Mechanical props, blocks or stands rated for the load protect anyone working beneath raised equipment.
### What personal protective equipment is needed for hydraulic work?
Typical PPE includes safety glasses or a face shield, suitable gloves, protective clothing and safety footwear. Tasks involving hot oil, high-pressure testing or work at height may require additional protection. Follow your site's risk assessments for each task.
